# Recovery Plan — Metabolic + Cardiac

> **Source document.** This is a faithful transcription of the original recovery
> plan, preserved as the seed content for the structured research base in this
> folder. It is a personal plan written with a specific cardiac picture in mind —
> **not** generic advice. The structured study built around it (see `BRIEF.md`)
> treats every lever here as a research entry to be mechanism-mapped and
> evidence-graded.

**Targets:** fatty liver (MASLD) → insulin resistance → obesity → heart health.

The core idea: these are **one connected problem with one master lever — lose
visceral fat.** Everything below serves that, while staying safe for a specific
cardiac picture (nebivolol, monomorphic PVCs, exaggerated BP response).

> **Clear with your cardiologist first:** the supplements (especially berberine)
> and the exercise intensity. Reasons are flagged in each section. Bring this whole
> list to your next Zan Mitrev appointment.

## 0. The master lever: fat loss

**Goal: lose 5–10% of body weight.** That alone meaningfully reverses liver fat
and improves insulin sensitivity. The mechanism is direct — weight loss shuts down
the liver's own fat-making pathway.

- **Pace:** ~0.5–1% of body weight per week. Slow and steady. Crash diets cost you
  muscle and can stress the liver short-term.
- **Track waist, not just weight.** Waist circumference is a better proxy for
  visceral/liver fat than the scale. Measure weekly, same time, same conditions.
- **Don't count obsessively.** Fix food quality and a few daily habits (below) and
  the deficit largely takes care of itself. Keep protein high so you lose fat, not
  muscle.

## 1. Supplements

Two foundational tiers + things to avoid. None of this replaces your prescribed
care, and the Tier 2 items genuinely need your cardiologist's sign-off because of
nebivolol + PVCs.

### Tier 1 — foundational, generally safe (still mention to your doctor)

- **Magnesium (glycinate or citrate)** — supports insulin sensitivity, sleep, and
  can reduce PVC frequency, especially if you run low. Glycinate is gentle on the
  gut. (~200–400 mg elemental/day.)
- **Omega-3 (EPA/DHA)** — lowers triglycerides, modestly helps liver fat, and is
  cardio-protective. (~1–2 g combined EPA + DHA/day.)
- **Vitamin D** — get your level tested first, then correct to target. Common
  deficiency, broad metabolic relevance.

### Tier 2 — strong evidence, but get cardiologist sign-off BEFORE starting

- **Berberine** — the most effective metabolic supplement here: solid evidence for
  insulin resistance, liver fat, and lipids (works via AMPK, like a gentle "natural
  metformin"). Why the caution: it has additive blood-pressure-lowering effects
  with your beta-blocker, is processed through the same liver enzymes as many
  drugs, and can influence heart rhythm/palpitations in some people. With your meds
  + PVCs, this is a doctor-approved-only item, not a self-start. (Studied at
  900–1500 mg/day, divided.)
- **Myo-inositol** — well-tolerated, supports insulin signaling. Lower-stakes than
  berberine but still worth a mention to your doctor. (~2–4 g/day.)

### Optional (for training, not metabolism)

- **Creatine monohydrate (3–5 g/day)** — helps strength and muscle (a bigger muscle
  mass = a bigger glucose "sink"). Safe for liver/kidneys in healthy people; expect
  a little water-weight. Not a metabolic agent — include only if you want the
  strength benefit.
- **Coffee** — counts as a supplement here: genuinely liver-protective (lower
  fibrosis risk in fatty liver). Keep it black or near-black. Just don't stack it
  with stimulant pre-workouts.

### Avoid / be careful (given liver + PVCs)

- **High-dose green tea extract (concentrated EGCG)** — liver-toxicity risk.
  Drinking green tea is fine; concentrated extract pills are not.
- **Stimulant "fat-burners" and high-caffeine pre-workouts** — can provoke PVCs and
  spike BP. Hard avoid.
- **Licorice root** (in some teas/supps) — raises blood pressure. Avoid.
- **Ashwagandha** — you flagged its rare hepatotoxicity yourself; not needed for
  these goals, so I'd leave it out while the liver is the priority.

## 2. Food allowed (Mediterranean base — the diet with the best evidence for all four)

Build meals around these. Skopje makes most of it easy and cheap.

- **Non-starchy vegetables** — abundant, every meal. Peppers, tomatoes, leafy
  greens, zucchini, eggplant, cucumber, onion, garlic.
- **Quality protein.** Fish (especially oily — sardines, mackerel), poultry, eggs,
  and legumes (beans, lentils, chickpeas — fiber + protein + low glycemic; tavče
  gravče with a light hand on the oil). Strained/Greek yogurt.
- **Healthy fats.** Extra-virgin olive oil as your main fat. Walnuts (local and
  excellent), other nuts, seeds, avocado.
- **Whole, intact grains in moderation.** Oats, barley, bulgur, quinoa,
  integral/whole-grain bread.
- **Fruit — whole, not juiced.** Berries especially; other fruit in sensible
  portions.
- **Drinks.** Water, green tea, black coffee.

## 3. Food to avoid (these are the actual drivers of liver fat + insulin resistance)

In rough order of impact:

1. **Added sugar — especially fructose.** Sodas, juice, sweetened drinks, pastries,
   desserts. Fructose is converted to fat directly in the liver. This is the single
   biggest one for fatty liver.
2. **Refined carbs.** White bread, daily burek, sugary cereals, lots of white
   flour. Spike insulin, feed fat storage.
3. **Ultra-processed foods.** Strongly linked to MASLD independent of calories.
4. **Alcohol.** For a fatty liver, ideally none or minimal. Even moderate alcohol
   plus metabolic dysfunction accelerates liver damage. Rakija counts.
5. **Excess salt.** Directly relevant to your exaggerated BP response. Watch
   processed/cured foods and added salt.
6. **Processed/cured meats** (sausages, kolbasi) — bad for both heart and
   metabolism. Occasional, not staple.

## 4. Activity

Both cardio and strength — they fix insulin resistance through different
mechanisms, so you want both.

### Cardio — your backbone

- **Zone 2** ("comfortably hard, can still hold a conversation"), ~150+ min/week,
  built up gradually. Best tool for fat-burning, mitochondrial health, and insulin
  sensitivity — and it's gentle on the heart. **Use RPE / the talk test, NOT
  heart-rate zones.** Nebivolol blunts your heart rate, so HR numbers will mislead
  you. Effort and breathing are your gauges.
- **Post-meal walks (10–15 min).** Quietly one of the most effective habits here —
  blunts the glucose spike after eating. Easy daily win, especially after your
  biggest meal.
- **HIIT / sprint intervals — only after cardiology clearance.** Big BP spikes are
  exactly what your stress test flagged. Park this until your cardiologist (and
  Holter) say go.

### Strength — 2–3x/week, full body

- Compound movements (squat/hinge/push/pull pattern). Building muscle creates a
  larger glucose sink and raises your resting metabolism.
- **Critical safety rule: breathe through every rep — no breath-holding (Valsalva),
  no maximal grinding sets.** Holding your breath under a heavy load spikes blood
  pressure hard, which is the one thing to avoid given your BP response and PVCs.
  Use moderate loads, controlled tempo, and leave 2–3 reps "in the tank." Progress
  slowly over weeks. The goal is consistency and joint-friendliness, not maxing out.

### Daily movement (you sit a lot — this matters)

- Break up long coding blocks: stand/walk a few minutes each hour. Aim for a daily
  step target you can actually hit, and nudge it up over time.

## 5. The supporting pillars (don't skip these)

- **Sleep.** Poor sleep wrecks insulin sensitivity and triggers PVCs. With obesity
  in the mix, ask your doctor about screening for sleep apnea — treating it can
  improve both the metabolic and the rhythm picture.
- **Stress / vagal load.** You've already researched your vagal PVC triggers — same
  logic applies: manage stress, don't over-caffeinate, watch late-night
  screen/work spikes.
- **Hydration.** Plain water as default; helps appetite control and supports the
  kidneys/liver.

## 6. Track this + loop in your medical team

- **Weekly:** weight, waist circumference, resting BP (you likely already log
  this), how your training RPE feels.
- **With Zan Mitrev:** review liver enzymes + metabolic panel over time; bring the
  supplement list to your cardiologist; let the Holter results set your
  exercise-intensity ceiling.
- **Ask your doctor about** (their call, not mine): whether a GLP-1 agonist fits
  your obesity/MASLD picture — these are increasingly used and could accelerate
  things, but they're prescription decisions on top of the lifestyle foundation,
  not a replacement for it.

## Week 1 — start today

1. Cut sugar-sweetened drinks and juice to zero. (Biggest single liver win.)
2. Add a 10-minute walk after your largest meal, daily.
3. Two full-body strength sessions this week — moderate, breathing through every rep.
4. Make olive oil + vegetables + a protein the template for most meals.
5. Book the supplement + intensity conversation with your cardiologist; until then,
   start only Tier 1 (magnesium, omega-3, vitamin D after testing).
