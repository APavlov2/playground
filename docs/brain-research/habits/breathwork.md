## Summary
Breathwork here means **slow-paced voluntary breathing** (roughly ~6 breaths/min / resonance-frequency breathing, the substrate of HRV biofeedback) and related pranayama, not hyperventilatory styles. Its most solid, replicated effect is **acute up-regulation of cardiac vagal (parasympathetic) tone** — measurable as increased HRV during and after practice. The downstream claims most relevant here (lower stress/anxiety, dampened HPA reactivity) are plausible and partly supported but rest on smaller, more heterogeneous data. `evidence_overall: 3` is anchored to the large, consistent meta-analytic evidence that slow breathing raises vagal tone; the specific cortisol/HPA and GABA endpoints are graded lower. Much of the broader "breathwork" wellness literature is emerging and lower quality than the focused slow-breathing/HRV work.

## Mechanism
- **hpa (primary, down).** Slowing the breath toward ~6 breaths/min maximizes respiratory sinus arrhythmia and baroreflex engagement, raising vagal (parasympathetic) outflow and shifting autonomic balance away from sympathetic dominance. The strongest evidence is for the autonomic step itself: a large meta-analysis (Laborde 2022, PMID:35623448) shows robust increases in cardiac vagal tone (e.g. RMSSD) across timepoints. The further claim — that this translates to reduced cortisol/HPA reactivity and lower subjective stress — is supported by the broader mind-body stress-marker literature (Pascoe 2017, PMID:28863392) but with smaller, less breathwork-specific effects, so the channel is graded for the well-established vagal mechanism with honest framing about the cortisol endpoint.
- **gaba (up, emerging).** The behavioral output is calm/anxiolysis. Some yogic-breathing work links practice to increased thalamic GABA and reduced anxiety, implicating inhibitory/GABAergic tone, but human pharmacodynamic evidence is indirect and sparse — graded emerging (2), `unsourced` at the level of a definitive human GABA-breathwork RCT.

## Evidence
**Vagal tone / HRV (best supported).** Laborde et al., *Neurosci Biobehav Rev*, 2022 (PMID:35623448) systematically reviewed 223 studies and found that voluntary slow breathing **reliably increases cardiac vagal tone** — during the session, immediately after a single session, and after multi-session interventions (e.g. a large RMSSD increase, SMD ~1.11, at the post-intervention timepoint). This is the firmest part of the breathwork story and the basis for the 3.

**Stress / anxiety / physiology.** The downstream affective and endocrine benefits draw on the broader mind-body stress-marker literature; Pascoe et al., 2017 (PMID:28863392) found small reductions in cortisol, blood pressure, and heart rate across meditation/mind-body practices that include paced breathing. Breathwork-specific RCTs on cortisol and clinical anxiety exist but are smaller, shorter, and more heterogeneous, so these endpoints are weaker than the autonomic one.

**Healthy vs clinical.** Acute vagal/HRV effects are demonstrable in healthy adults. Clinical anxiety/stress benefits are reported in stressed and patient populations but with the usual small-trial caveats. Cognitive-performance claims for breathwork are largely unproven and should not be over-stated.

Net: best-supported, most relevant claim — slow-paced breathing acutely and durably increases cardiac vagal tone — rates **Good (3)**. The cortisol/HPA and GABA endpoints are **Emerging (2)**.

## Safety & interactions (research metadata)
Slow-paced breathing is low-risk for most people. The notable hazard is **not** slow breathing but **hyperventilatory/fast styles** (holotropic, Wim-Hof-type, intense pranayama with breath retention): these cause hypocapnia and can produce lightheadedness, paresthesia, carpopedal spasm/tetany, and **syncope** — dangerous near water or when standing. Caution in cardiovascular disease, uncontrolled hypertension, pregnancy, epilepsy, and panic disorder (paradoxical anxiety). No drug interactions. This is research metadata, not medical advice.

## Open questions
- Does the reliable acute vagal-tone increase translate into **durable** reductions in clinically meaningful anxiety, cortisol, and allostatic load, or is much of it state-only?
- Optimal **dose** (breaths/min, minutes/day, weeks) and whether individualized resonance-frequency breathing outperforms a fixed ~6/min cadence.
- Mechanistic confirmation of a **GABAergic** contribution in humans (currently indirect/`unsourced` at RCT level).
- How much of "breathwork" wellness benefit is specific to the breathing vs general relaxation/expectancy — active-control trials are scarce.
- Real-world adverse-event rates for intensive hyperventilatory protocols, which are poorly quantified.
