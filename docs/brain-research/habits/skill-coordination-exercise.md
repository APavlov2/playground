## Summary
Skill- and coordination-based exercise (dance, racket sports, motor-learning routines) layers continuous motor learning, novelty, attention and rhythm-motor integration on top of an aerobic stimulus — an "enriched-environment" form of exercise. The standout human finding is from a dance RCT in older adults: 18 months of learning varied dance routines increased hippocampal volume and raised BDNF, and uniquely improved balance, compared with repetitive endurance training. Mechanistically it shares the neurotrophic and perfusion pathways of aerobic exercise (ntrophic, cbf) but adds use-dependent synaptic/motor-learning plasticity (glu) and engages cerebellar and cortical motor networks. Evidence is good for cognition/structure in older adults; whether the skill component adds reliably beyond matched aerobic dose is the key open question.

## Mechanism
**ntrophic (primary, up).** Skill/coordination exercise combines aerobic load with motor learning, novelty and rhythm-motor integration, approximating an enriched environment — a stimulus that, in preclinical work, strongly drives hippocampal neurogenesis and BDNF. In humans, Rehfeld 2017 (PMID:28674488) found that 18 months of dance increased hippocampal volume in older adults, and a related report found dance raised BDNF more than conventional repetitive training. Graded **3** on a direct human structural RCT plus the broader exercise-BDNF literature.

**glu (modulate).** Motor-skill acquisition is the canonical driver of use-dependent synaptic plasticity / LTP in motor cortex, cerebellum and hippocampus — the cellular substrate of learning. Novel coordinative demand should engage glutamatergic plasticity beyond what repetitive cardio does. The general mechanism is well established, but skill-exercise-specific human LTP evidence is indirect, so **2** and direction modulate (plasticity, not blunt upregulation).

**cbf (up).** The aerobic component raises cardiac output, perfusion and shear stress as in other aerobic exercise (shared with Erickson 2011, PMID:21282661, and the cerebrovascular-fitness literature), while coordinative demand additionally recruits cerebellar/cortical motor networks. Graded **3** on the shared aerobic-perfusion mechanism.

**inflam (down).** Regular activity lowers systemic inflammatory markers (CRP, IL-6); not specific to the coordination component and inferred from peripheral markers, so **2**.

## Evidence
- **Rehfeld K et al., 2017 (PMID:28674488)** — RCT in healthy seniors (mean age ~68) comparing 18 months of weekly novel dance routines against repetitive endurance/flexibility training. Both groups increased hippocampal volume, but only the dance group showed improved balance, and related analyses reported larger BDNF increases with dance. This is the most direct human evidence that the skill/coordination component adds behavioral and plasticity benefit, anchoring the **3** grade.
- **Northey JM et al., 2018 (PMID:28438770)** — meta-analysis of exercise interventions for cognition in adults over 50; exercise (including modalities with coordinative content such as tai chi) improved cognition. Aggregate support for the broader category.
- **Ngandu T et al. (FINGER), 2015 (PMID:25771249)** — large multidomain RCT (diet, exercise, cognitive training, vascular monitoring) that improved/maintained cognition in at-risk elderly; relevant because it combines physical and cognitive/skill challenge, the same convergence skill-exercise embodies, though it does not isolate coordinative exercise.
- **Erickson KI et al., 2011 (PMID:21282661)** — cited for the shared aerobic ntrophic/cbf mechanism (hippocampal volume increase with aerobic training), which skill exercise inherits via its aerobic component.

Net: best-supported, most relevant claim — coordination-rich exercise (dance) increases hippocampal volume and improves balance/cognition in older adults — rates **Good (3)** (a direct RCT plus supporting meta-analytic and multidomain-trial evidence). Not 4 because the dance-specific RCT base is small and the incremental benefit of the skill component over matched aerobic dose is not yet firmly meta-analyzed.

## Safety & interactions (research metadata)
Generally safe and engaging, which aids adherence. Injury metadata centers on the dynamic, coordinative nature of the activity: falls and acute musculoskeletal injury (sprains, racket-sport ankle/knee injuries), with supervision advised in those with severe balance impairment or high fall risk; overuse injury at high volume; and the usual cardiovascular-clearance caveat for vigorous dance/sport in unstable cardiovascular disease. No pharmacological interactions. Research metadata, not medical advice.

## Open questions
- Does the skill/coordination component add reliably beyond a matched aerobic dose, or is the benefit largely the aerobic stimulus plus better adherence/enjoyment? Rehfeld is suggestive but small.
- Which coordinative demands matter most (balance, sequence learning, social/musical engagement) and do they map onto distinct channels (cerebellar vs hippocampal)?
- Generalization across activities — does racket sport confer the same brain benefits as partnered dance? Direct head-to-head data are sparse.
- Durability of structural/connectivity changes and dose-response (frequency, novelty rate, program length).
- Whether the glutamatergic/motor-learning plasticity mechanism is measurable in humans for skill exercise specifically, beyond inference from motor-learning paradigms.
