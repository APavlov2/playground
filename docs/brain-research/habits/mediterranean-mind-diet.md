## Summary
The Mediterranean and MIND (Mediterranean-DASH Intervention for Neurodegenerative Delay) patterns are whole-food dietary patterns built on vegetables, leafy greens, berries, fish, olive oil, nuts, legumes and whole grains, with low red meat, fried food and sweets. Among all dietary levers in this study this pattern has the **strongest human evidence for cognition and dementia risk** — large prospective cohorts show slower cognitive decline and lower Alzheimer's incidence with higher adherence, and it is the diet arm of the landmark FINGER multidomain prevention trial. The honest ceiling is that the dedicated 3-year MIND RCT (Barnes 2023) was essentially null versus an active comparator, so this is graded **Good (3)**, not Strong, and the effect is best read as population-level risk reduction over years rather than a measurable acute cognitive boost.

## Mechanism
- **inflam (primary, down).** The pattern is dense in polyphenols, monounsaturated fat (olive oil), and marine omega-3 while low in refined carbohydrate and red/processed meat. The net effect is reduced systemic and central oxidative/inflammatory tone (lower CRP/IL-6, less microglial priming and ROS load). This anti-inflammatory/antioxidant signature is the most mechanistically coherent route from "what you eat" to "slower neurodegeneration."
- **cbf (vascular, up).** Much of the dementia-risk benefit is plausibly vascular. Adherence improves blood pressure, LDL, endothelial function and insulin sensitivity, which preserves cerebral perfusion and limits cerebral small-vessel disease and white-matter hyperintensities — a major substrate of late-life cognitive decline. Nitrate- and polyphenol-rich foods additionally support NO-mediated endothelial vasodilation (see polyphenol-rich-intake entry, with which this overlaps heavily).
- **glu (indirect, modulate).** Better metabolic and vascular substrate plus lower neuroinflammation is permissive for hippocampal synaptic integrity, but there is no direct human demonstration that the pattern modulates glutamatergic signalling or LTP. Graded **1 / preclinical** and listed only for mechanistic completeness — not a load-bearing claim.

## Evidence
Human evidence is the best of any dietary lever here, but it is heavier on the observational side than the RCT side.

- **Cohort — Alzheimer's incidence.** Morris 2015 (PMID 25681666) in the Rush Memory and Aging Project (n=923, ~4.5 y) reported that highest-tertile MIND adherence was associated with markedly lower Alzheimer's incidence (hazard ratio roughly 0.47 vs lowest tertile), and even moderate adherence showed benefit — notably the MIND pattern outperformed strict Mediterranean or DASH adherence for AD risk in that cohort.
- **Cohort — rate of decline.** Morris 2015 (PMID 26086182, n=960, ~4.7 y) found higher MIND adherence associated with slower global cognitive decline, with the top-vs-bottom tertile difference described as equivalent to being ~7.5 years younger. These are consistent observational signals but cannot exclude residual confounding by overall healthy-lifestyle clustering.
- **RCT — the honest correction.** Barnes 2023 (PMID 37466280), a 3-year, two-site RCT in 604 older adults at risk (family history, BMI >25), found **no significant difference** in the cognitive composite between the MIND diet and a mild-caloric-restriction control diet — both groups improved. This is the key down-grading result: a rigorous trial against an active comparator did not reproduce the cohort effect size. It does not refute long-term benefit (3 years is short for dementia endpoints, and the control was itself a reasonable diet), but it caps the grade at 3.
- **RCT — Mediterranean cognition.** Valls-Pedret 2015 (PMID 25961184), a PREDIMED substudy (n=447), found Mediterranean diet supplemented with olive oil or nuts improved or preserved cognition versus a low-fat control over ~4 years — supportive RCT evidence for the broader Mediterranean pattern, though within a trial designed primarily for cardiovascular endpoints.
- **Multidomain — FINGER.** Ngandu 2015 (PMID 25771249), the FINGER RCT (n=1260, 2 y), bundled dietary counselling (Mediterranean/Nordic-style) with exercise, cognitive training and vascular monitoring and showed a small but significant benefit on a cognitive composite. Diet was one of four interacting components, so FINGER supports diet-as-part-of-a-package rather than isolating the diet's effect.

Net: best-supported, most relevant claim — higher adherence to a Mediterranean/MIND pattern is associated with slower cognitive decline and lower dementia/AD risk over years — rates **Good (3)**, with explicit acknowledgement that the dedicated MIND RCT was null versus an active comparator.

## Safety & interactions (research metadata)
This is a whole-food eating pattern with no notable risk profile. Research-metadata caveats: high and *variable* leafy-green (vitamin K) intake can destabilise warfarin dosing (consistency matters more than avoidance); increased fish intake carries the standard mercury caution for large predatory species, especially in pregnancy. The dominant interpretive caveat is not safety but **confounding** — in observational data, people who eat this way differ systematically (education, activity, smoking) from those who do not, which inflates effect sizes relative to RCTs. This is research metadata, not dietary advice.

## Open questions
- Why did the 3-year MIND RCT (Barnes 2023) fail to beat an active comparator when cohorts show large effects? Insufficient duration, ceiling from an already-decent control diet, or genuinely smaller causal effect?
- How much of the benefit is the *pattern* versus its components (olive oil, fish-derived DHA, berry/leafy-green polyphenols) — i.e. is there an irreducible synergy, or is it additive?
- How much of the cohort signal is vascular (cbf) versus direct anti-inflammatory neuroprotection (inflam)? The two are hard to disentangle in humans.
- Does adherence in midlife (vs late life) matter more for dementia endpoints? Most RCT exposure windows are short and late.
- Effect modification by APOE genotype and baseline diet quality is suggested but not settled.
