## Summary

Dihexa (N-hexanoic-Tyr-Ile-(6) aminohexanoic amide; PNB-0408) is a synthetic, metabolically
stabilized oligopeptide derived from angiotensin IV, developed by John Wright and Joseph Harding
at Washington State University. In preclinical rodent models it has been reported as a potent
procognitive and synaptogenic agent, with a proposed mechanism of potentiating hepatocyte growth
factor (HGF) / c-Met receptor signaling to drive hippocampal spinogenesis and synaptogenesis. It
is an EXPERIMENTAL research chemical with no approved use and essentially no human data. Overall
evidence is graded PRECLINICAL (1). The most-cited mechanistic study was later retracted, which
further weakens an already human-data-free evidence base.

## Mechanism

- Neurotrophic / HGF–c-Met potentiation (ntrophic): Dihexa is proposed to bind HGF with high
  affinity and facilitate c-Met receptor activation at subthreshold HGF concentrations, augmenting
  HGF-dependent signaling and inducing hippocampal spinogenesis and synaptogenesis in rodent
  preparations; reported effects were blocked by an HGF antagonist and by c-Met knockdown
  (preclinical). The principal paper making this mechanistic claim (PMID: 25187433) has since been
  RETRACTED, so the mechanism should be regarded as proposed but NOT reliably established.
- Glutamatergic/synaptic connectivity (glu): The synaptogenic and spinogenic actions imply an
  increase in excitatory synaptic connections downstream of c-Met-driven dendritic arborization.
  This channel is indirect/inferred from the synaptogenesis claims rather than directly measured
  glutamatergic pharmacology, and is graded accordingly (preclinical, evidence 1).

## Evidence

Grade: PRECLINICAL (evidence_overall 1). All efficacy data are rodent. NO human data exist.

- Procognitive rodent data (ntrophic, evidence 1): Dihexa was reported to reverse cognitive
  deficits in two rodent dementia models — scopolamine-induced amnesia and aged rats — improving
  Morris water-maze performance after intracerebroventricular or oral-gavage dosing (PMID: 23055539).
  IMPORTANT: this paper carries a 2021 Notice of Concern (PMID: 34551989); treat its conclusions
  cautiously.
- Mechanism (HGF/c-Met) (ntrophic, evidence 1): The synaptogenesis/HGF-dependence mechanism was
  reported in PMID: 25187433, which was RETRACTED in 2025 (retraction notice PMID: 40312093). This
  removes the primary mechanistic support; the proposed HGF/c-Met pathway is therefore not reliably
  established.
- Review / context: A non-retracted review by the originating group summarizes the AngIV-analog
  program, including dihexa's design for metabolic stability, oral activity, and BBB permeability,
  and its proposed IRAP and HGF/c-Met mechanisms in Alzheimer's and Parkinson's models
  (PMID: 25455861). This is a narrative review by the developers, not independent confirmation.
- HUMAN DATA: ABSENT. No clinical trial, no human safety dataset, and no human efficacy data for
  dihexa were identified. Any claim of human cognitive benefit, human dosing, or human
  pharmacokinetics is (unsourced).
- Independent replication: Independent, non-developer replication of the key synaptogenesis findings
  is not established in the verified literature reviewed here (unsourced).

## Safety & interactions (research metadata)

HUMAN SAFETY IS UNKNOWN. No human safety dataset, no toxicology in humans, no long-term data. This
section is research metadata, not medical advice.

- Oncogenic theoretical concern: The proposed mechanism — activation/potentiation of HGF/c-Met
  signaling — overlaps directly with a proto-oncogenic pathway. c-Met activation is mechanistically
  associated with tumor proliferation, invasion, and metastasis. Whether chronic dihexa exposure
  promotes neoplasia in humans is UNKNOWN; this is a genuine, unresolved theoretical risk that
  warrants explicit caution (mechanistic concern).
- Contraindications: not established for any human use (no approved indication); pregnancy/lactation
  no data; personal/family cancer history flagged on theoretical grounds (all uncharacterized).
- Interactions: no characterized human drug interactions; theoretical additive effects with
  growth-factor/c-Met-modulating agents (uncharacterized).
- Supply/quality: unregulated research-chemical market — identity, purity, sterility, and dose
  accuracy are not assured (unsourced).
