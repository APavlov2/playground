## Summary
Alpha-lipoic acid (ALA, thioctic acid) is an endogenous, sulfur-containing dithiol compound that is both an essential mitochondrial cofactor (for the pyruvate dehydrogenase and alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase complexes) and, in supplemental form, a redox-active antioxidant that regenerates glutathione, vitamin C and vitamin E. Its strongest human evidence by far is for **diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN)** — a *peripheral nerve* indication, not cognition — where IV 600 mg/day for 3 weeks produces statistically significant short-term symptom relief in pooled trials (ALADIN, SYDNEY, NATHAN II). That neuropathy signal must **not** be read across to brain/cognition: human central-cognition data are thin, confined to a few small Alzheimer's-disease pilots (usually ALA combined with omega-3), with no positive RCT in healthy adults. Overall cognitive evidence is therefore graded **Weak (2)**, driven down by absence of healthy-population data and by confounding in the AD pilots.

## Mechanism
**mito (cofactor, up).** ALA, as protein-bound lipoamide, is an obligatory cofactor of the mitochondrial 2-oxoacid dehydrogenase complexes — pyruvate dehydrogenase (linking glycolysis to the TCA cycle) and alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase — and of the glycine cleavage system. This biochemical role is firmly established. Supplemental free ALA additionally enters cells, is reduced to dihydrolipoic acid (DHLA), and modulates cellular redox/bioenergetic state. The leap that is **not** established: that exogenous ALA improves human brain bioenergetics in a way that translates to measurable cognitive gain. Hence the channel mechanism is real but the cognitive payoff is unproven — graded conservatively (2).

**inflam (antioxidant, down).** ALA/DHLA is one of the few amphipathic ("universal") antioxidants, active in both lipid and aqueous compartments. It directly scavenges reactive oxygen species, chelates redox-active metals (Fe, Cu), regenerates oxidised glutathione, ascorbate and tocopherol, and downregulates NF-kB-driven inflammatory signalling (multiple reviews; mechanism robust in vitro and in metabolic/peripheral-nerve models). In DPN this antioxidant action is the proposed basis for symptom relief. Central anti-neuroinflammatory benefit relevant to cognition in non-diseased brains remains hypothetical, so this channel is also graded 2.

## Evidence
**Peripheral neuropathy (strong, but NOT cognition).** The best-quality human evidence is for diabetic polyneuropathy. Ziegler et al. 2004 (PMID:14984445) meta-analysed four RCTs (ALADIN I, ALADIN III, SYDNEY, NATHAN II; ~1,258 patients) of 600 mg/day IV ALA for 3 weeks and found a significant, clinically meaningful reduction in Total Symptom Score versus placebo (~24% relative improvement) plus improvement on the Neuropathy Impairment Score. This is a genuine grade-A-type signal — but it is *peripheral sensory-nerve symptom relief*, not central cognition, and the most consistent benefit is for the IV (not oral) regimen. The 2024 Cochrane review (Baicus et al., PMID:38205823; DOI:10.1002/14651858.CD012967.pub2) appraised ALA as a disease-modifying agent in DPN more cautiously, emphasising low-certainty evidence and unclear long-term/clinical relevance, especially for oral dosing. Net: peripheral-nerve evidence is real but should be reported honestly as low-to-moderate certainty and channel-irrelevant to brain cognition.

**Central cognition (thin).** No RCT shows a cognitive benefit of ALA in healthy adults. The central-cognition literature is essentially a handful of small Alzheimer's-disease studies. The main controlled one, Shinto et al. 2014 (PMID:24077434), randomized only 39 AD patients to omega-3, omega-3 + ALA, or placebo for 12 months: the omega-3 + ALA arm showed less decline on MMSE (p<0.01) and IADL, but there was **no** difference on the primary cognitive instrument ADAS-cog, the sample was tiny, and ALA was **confounded with omega-3** (no ALA-alone arm). Older open-label/uncontrolled reports (ALA added to standard AD care) are hypothesis-generating at best. The Alzheimer's Drug Discovery Foundation's appraisal likewise notes no RCT has shown ALA prevents or treats dementia.

Net: cognition evidence = **Weak (2)** and graded down explicitly because (a) no healthy-adult RCT exists, (b) the only controlled AD signal is small, confounded with omega-3, and null on its primary cognitive endpoint, and (c) the robust DPN data are peripheral and must not inflate the cognitive grade. evidence_overall: 2.

## Safety & interactions (research metadata)
Generally well tolerated at studied doses; commonest effects are GI upset and rash.
- **Hypoglycemia / insulin:** ALA can enhance insulin sensitivity and glucose uptake and may add to the glucose-lowering effect of insulin and insulin secretagogues (sulfonylureas), so combined use raises hypoglycemia risk. Regulatory safety reviews (e.g. Health Canada) also flag **insulin autoimmune syndrome (IAS)** causing hypoglycemia in genetically predisposed individuals (HLA-DR4-associated, more common in East-Asian populations); cases resolved on discontinuation.
- **Biotin / minerals:** ALA may interfere with biotin uptake/pathways (high-dose biotin co-supplementation sometimes advised) and can chelate metal ions (iron, zinc, copper), so it is typically separated from mineral supplements by a few hours.
- **Thyroid medication:** theoretical interaction (unsourced for clinical significance).
This is research metadata, not medical advice.

## Open questions
- Does ALA produce ANY measurable cognitive benefit in healthy adults? No RCT has tested this directly — current grade reflects absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.
- Is the AD signal in Shinto 2014 attributable to ALA at all, given the omega-3 confound and tiny n? An ALA-monotherapy AD RCT is missing.
- Does the well-established mito-cofactor / antioxidant mechanism translate to central bioenergetic or anti-neuroinflammatory effects in vivo in humans, or does poor oral bioavailability (~30-40%, ~30 min half-life) and rapid clearance blunt central exposure?
- Oral vs IV: DPN benefit is clearest with IV; whether oral dosing reaches efficacious central or even peripheral exposure is unresolved (Cochrane caution).
- R- vs racemic ALA and stabilised/sustained-release formulations — pharmacokinetic improvements exist, but cognitive endpoints remain untested.
