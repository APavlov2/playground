## Summary

Gotu kola (Centella asiatica) is an herbal extract whose active triterpenoid saponins
(asiaticoside, asiatic acid, madecassoside) drive an attractive but **largely preclinical**
neuro-regenerative story. Human evidence is thin: a few small RCTs and a 2017 systematic
review/meta-analysis (11 RCTs) found **no significant benefit across cognitive domains**
versus placebo, with only modest acute mood/alertness signals (PMID: 28878245). The most
reproducible human signal is **acute anxiolytic** (attenuated acoustic startle, PMID: 11106141)
and small acute mood/working-memory changes (PMID: 18191355). Overall grade is **LOW
(evidence_overall: 2)**: the mechanism is compelling in cells and rodents but unconfirmed in
humans, human RCTs are few/small/heterogeneous, and there are rare but real hepatotoxicity
case reports.

**evidence_overall: 2** — justification: there are *some* human RCTs (so not a pure
preclinical "1"), but they are few, small, methodologically heterogeneous, and predominantly
null for cognition; the strongest mechanistic weight is preclinical-only, which caps the grade
at 2 per honest-grading rules. It does not reach 3 (no consistent, replicated human cognitive
benefit; no Cochrane-grade sufficiency).

## Mechanism

- **Neurotrophic / structural (ntrophic, preclinical, evidence 1, up):** Centella asiatica
  extract and isolated triterpenoids promote neurite outgrowth, axodendritic maturation,
  synapse formation, and dendritic arborization / spine density in primary neurons and rodent
  hippocampus (PMID: 33444677; PMID: 18218554; PMID: 28279707; PMID: 29719882). Some
  neuritogenic activity is reported through TrkA/neurotrophin-linked signaling. BDNF upregulation
  is described in rodent models but is **not confirmed by any human biomarker** — treat the
  BDNF/TrkA claim as preclinical only.
- **Antioxidant / anti-inflammatory & anti-amyloid (inflam, preclinical, evidence 1, down):**
  In cell and rodent models the extract reduces oxidative stress and Abeta-associated
  neurodegenerative changes (PMID: 28279707). No human clinical-cognitive demonstration of this
  mechanism.
- **Anxiolytic / stress reactivity (hpa, healthy, evidence 2, modulate; gaba, preclinical,
  evidence 1, modulate):** A single 12 g dose attenuated peak acoustic startle response at 30
  and 60 min in healthy adults (PMID: 11106141). A GABAergic basis is hypothesized from
  preclinical anxiety models but is **not directly demonstrated in humans** (mechanistic
  attribution largely unsourced for the human channel).

## Evidence

**Human RCTs / systematic review (the controlling evidence):**

- **Systematic review & meta-analysis (11 RCTs)** — Puttarak et al., Sci Rep 2017,
  PMID: 28878245 (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-017-09823-9): **no significant difference in any
  cognitive domain** vs placebo. Signals limited to acute mood — increased alertness and
  decreased anger ~1 h post-dose. Authors flag heterogeneity in dose, preparation, and
  standardization and call for properly designed trials. This is the single most important
  honesty anchor: pooled human cognition data are essentially null.
- **Healthy elderly RCT** — Wattanathorn et al., J Ethnopharmacol 2008, PMID: 18191355:
  double-blind, placebo-controlled, n=28, doses 250/500/750 mg/day x 2 months. Only the high
  dose enhanced **working memory** and increased N100 ERP amplitude; small sample, single
  trial, multiple comparisons.
- **Acute anxiolytic RCT** — Bradwejn et al., J Clin Psychopharmacol 2000, PMID: 11106141:
  double-blind placebo-controlled; single 12 g dose significantly attenuated peak acoustic
  startle amplitude at 30 and 60 min. Demonstrates an **acute** effect, not chronic cognitive
  benefit.
- **Post-stroke vascular cognitive impairment RCT** — Farhana et al., Evid Based Complement
  Alternat Med 2016, PMID: 27340413: Gotu kola 750 mg (n=17) or 1000 mg (n=17) vs folic acid
  3 mg (n=14) x 6 weeks; **all groups improved comparably** on MoCA-Ina, with a memory-domain
  edge for gotu kola. Small, active-comparator, short duration; improvements plausibly reflect
  natural post-stroke recovery.

**Cochrane / sufficiency note:** No dedicated Cochrane review establishing efficacy in dementia
was located on PubMed; the available systematic-review evidence is judged **insufficient** to
support a cognitive benefit, and **no study has shown** gotu kola slows decline in dementia.
(The prompt's "Cochrane review found insufficient evidence" framing aligns with the overall
insufficiency conclusion; the specific source identified here is the 2017 Sci Rep meta-analysis,
PMID: 28878245, not a verified Cochrane review — labeling the Cochrane attribution as
**(unsourced)**.)

**Preclinical weight (does not raise the human grade):** Robust, repeated cell/rodent evidence
for neurite outgrowth, dendritic arborization, synaptogenesis, antioxidant and anti-amyloid
effects (PMIDs 33444677, 18218554, 28279707, 29719882). This is mechanistically encouraging but,
per grading rules, **preclinical-only mechanisms cap evidence at 2** and cannot substitute for
human efficacy.

**Population distinction:** In *healthy* adults the signal is acute (mood/alertness, startle
attenuation, one working-memory finding). In *impaired* populations (post-stroke, dementia)
there is no convincing extract-specific cognitive benefit beyond comparator/natural recovery.
Hence population is set to **both** at the compound level, with channel-level populations
specified (preclinical for ntrophic/inflam/gaba; healthy for hpa).

## Safety & interactions

- **Hepatotoxicity (rare but real):** Case reports describe idiosyncratic liver injury,
  including in an adult series (PMID: 15801887) and a **pediatric case** (PMID: 21334992).
  Avoid in pre-existing liver disease; counsel users to stop and seek evaluation for symptoms
  of hepatic injury (jaundice, dark urine, RUQ pain, fatigue).
- **Sedation/drowsiness:** Consistent with the anxiolytic profile; theoretical additive effect
  with CNS depressants and sedatives.
- **Theoretical additive hepatic risk** with other hepatotoxic agents (e.g. acetaminophen,
  statins) — precautionary, not directly demonstrated.
- **Product variability:** Extract standardization, plant part, and preparation differ widely
  across trials, undermining cross-study comparability and dosing guidance.
- **Name confusion:** "Brahmi" is used for both Centella asiatica and Bacopa monnieri; verify
  the actual species on any product.

## Open questions

- Is there any reproducible **chronic** cognitive benefit in healthy adults, or is the human
  signal limited to acute mood/anxiety?
- Do the preclinical **BDNF/TrkA/neurotrophic and dendritic** effects translate to any human
  biomarker or imaging endpoint? (Currently unconfirmed.)
- What standardized triterpenoid dose (asiaticoside/asiatic acid/madecassoside content) and
  duration, if any, are needed for a cognitive effect? Current trials are not comparable.
- Human CNS **pharmacokinetics / half-life** of the active triterpenoids are poorly defined.
- True incidence and mechanism of **hepatotoxicity**, and which preparations/contaminants drive
  it.
