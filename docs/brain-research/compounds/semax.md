> WARNING - RESEARCH-GRADE / REGULATORY STATUS: Semax is a prescription drug in Russia
> (approved by the Russian Ministry of Health for cerebrovascular/cognitive indications) but is
> NOT approved by the FDA, EMA, or other major Western regulators. Outside Russia it is
> experimental/unapproved and is typically sold as a gray-market "research" peptide of unverified
> identity and purity. Nearly all evidence is preclinical or from small Russian-language clinical
> reports; Western RCT evidence is essentially absent. Treat all efficacy claims as unproven.

## Summary

Semax is a synthetic heptapeptide (Met-Glu-His-Phe-Pro-Gly-Pro) derived from the ACTH(4-10)
fragment, with a C-terminal Pro-Gly-Pro extension intended to slow enzymatic degradation. It is
administered intranasally. In Russia it has been used clinically for several decades for ischemic
stroke, cognitive disorders, and certain optic-nerve conditions. The mechanistic rationale centers
on upregulation of neurotrophins (BDNF, NGF) and modulation of dopaminergic and neuroinflammatory
pathways. However, the human efficacy evidence base is small, largely Russian-language, and
frequently open-label; controlled Western trials are essentially absent. Overall evidence is graded
LOW (2). Many commonly repeated claims about onset, dosing, and human pharmacokinetics are not
substantiated in peer-reviewed English literature and are marked (unsourced).

## Mechanism

- Neurotrophic upregulation (ntrophic): In rodents, single intranasal Semax increased hippocampal
  BDNF protein and trkB phosphorylation and raised exon-III BDNF and trkB mRNA (PMID: 16996037).
  Semax also altered neurotrophin gene expression in rat brain (PMID: 17353092). After cerebral
  ischemia, Semax (and its metabolite Pro-Gly-Pro) has been reported to activate transcription of
  neurotrophins and their receptors (preclinical).
- Dopaminergic modulation (da): Semax reduced MPTP-induced behavioral disturbances in rats, an
  effect attributed to combined dopaminergic modulation and neurotrophic action (PMID: 15341218).
  Reports that Semax augments psychostimulant-driven dopamine release derive from animal studies.
- Neuroinflammation (inflam): In rodent cerebral ischemia-reperfusion models, transcriptomic studies
  report suppression of inflammatory gene networks and reduced pro-inflammatory mediators
  (preclinical signal; specific cytokine magnitudes for human use are unsourced).
- Proposed metabolite mechanism: The Pro-Gly-Pro extension is thought to inhibit enkephalinase and
  extend functional duration relative to the bare ACTH(4-10) fragment; precise human pharmacokinetics
  and the dominance of the Pro-Gly-Pro metabolite in human tissue are (unsourced) in the verified
  English literature reviewed here.

## Evidence

Grade: LOW (evidence_overall 2). Strongest data are PRECLINICAL; human data are limited and
predominantly Russian.

- Preclinical neurotrophic (ntrophic, evidence 2): Rodent studies consistently report BDNF/NGF/trkB
  upregulation after Semax (PMID: 16996037; PMID: 17353092). These are animal molecular/behavioral
  studies, not human outcomes.
- Preclinical dopaminergic / neuroprotection (da, evidence 1): MPTP rat model shows attenuation of
  dopaminergic-lesion behavioral deficits (PMID: 15341218). Single model, low translation confidence.
- Preclinical inflammation (inflam, evidence 1): Anti-inflammatory/transcriptomic signals in rodent
  ischemia models (preclinical). Magnitudes and human relevance unverified here (partly unsourced).
- Preclinical antidepressant/antistress: Semax showed antidepressant-like and antistress effects in a
  rat chronic-unpredictable-stress model, with hippocampal BDNF involvement (PMID: 39442746). Animal
  only; human antidepressant efficacy is (unsourced).
- Russian clinical use (stroke/cognitive): A Russian-language clinical report describes efficacy of
  Semax across stages of ischemic stroke (PMID: 29798983, [Article in Russian]). Such reports are
  often open-label or methodologically limited and have NOT been independently replicated in
  controlled Western trials.
- WESTERN RCT EVIDENCE: Essentially ABSENT. No high-quality randomized controlled trial in healthy
  cognitive enhancement or in any indication was identified in the verified English literature; any
  claim of proven human cognitive benefit is (unsourced).

## Safety & interactions

Safety data are SPARSE. There is no Western RCT-grade adverse-event dataset; long-term human safety,
healthy-user safety, and intranasal local tolerability are not well characterized in peer-reviewed
English literature (largely unsourced).

- Contraindications: pregnancy/lactation (insufficient data, unsourced); known peptide hypersensitivity
  (label-level, unsourced). Use in acute agitated/psychiatric states uncharacterized (unsourced).
- Interactions: animal data suggest Semax can augment psychostimulant effects on central dopamine
  release; clinical significance unknown. Interactions with other CNS-active drugs are not
  characterized (unsourced).
- Notable risks: gray-market/research-grade supply means identity, purity, sterility, and dose accuracy
  are not assured. Reported side-effect profile in Russian use is described as mild, but rigorous
  Western pharmacovigilance is absent (unsourced).

## Open questions

- Does the rodent BDNF/NGF upregulation translate to meaningful human cognitive or recovery outcomes?
  (Unproven; no Western RCT.)
- What are validated human pharmacokinetics (parent peptide vs. Pro-Gly-Pro metabolite), bioavailability,
  and dose-response after intranasal dosing? (Largely unsourced in verified English literature.)
- Is there any benefit in HEALTHY users, as opposed to ischemic/impaired populations? (Unstudied;
  unsourced.)
- What is the long-term safety profile, including repeated intranasal use? (Unsourced.)
- Can the Russian stroke/cognitive findings be replicated under blinded, controlled, registered trials?
  (Open; not yet done in the West.)
