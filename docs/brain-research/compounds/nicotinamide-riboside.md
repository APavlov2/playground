## Summary

Nicotinamide riboside (NR; e.g. Niagen) is an NAD+ precursor that **reliably and dose-dependently raises blood NAD+** in human RCTs — this is one of the best-established biomarker effects of any nootropic-adjacent supplement (Trammell 2016, PMID 27721479; Martens 2018, PMID 29599478; Conze 2019, PMID 31278280). However, the **downstream clinical and cognitive benefit is UNPROVEN**. The two existing cognition RCTs both showed NAD+ elevation but **no significant cognitive benefit versus placebo** (PMID 39817194; PMID 41357333). Brain claims are graded LOW. The key honest distinction: "raises NAD+" (proven biomarker) is NOT the same as "improves brain function" (not demonstrated). Generally well tolerated short-term; long-term safety unknown.

## Mechanism

NR enters the NAD+ salvage pathway (via NRK1/NRK2 kinases to NMN, then to NAD+), elevating the cellular NAD+ metabolome. NAD+ is a cofactor for redox reactions (bioenergetics), sirtuins, and PARPs, so the proposed brain rationale is improved mitochondrial function and bioenergetic resilience (mito channel). Preclinically, NAD+ repletion supports mitochondrial biogenesis and neuronal resilience, which underpins a speculative neurotrophic angle (ntrophic channel, preclinical only). Critically, the mechanistic plausibility does NOT establish a cognitive effect: the human trials show the biomarker moves without the clinical endpoint following.

## Evidence

**NAD+ elevation — PROVEN (biomarker):**
- Trammell 2016 (PMID 27721479): first human PK trial; single oral NR doses (100/300/1000 mg) produced dose-dependent increases in the blood NAD+ metabolome.
- Conze 2019 (PMID 31278280): randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 140 healthy overweight adults, 8 weeks; 100/300/1000 mg/day raised blood NAD+ dose-dependently (~22-142%), sustained through week 8, no flushing.
- Martens 2018 (PMID 29599478): randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover, healthy middle-aged/older adults; 1000 mg/day raised NAD+ ~60%; suggested (not proven) BP/arterial-stiffness benefit.
- NADPARK (PMID 35235774): 1000 mg/day for 30 days raised **cerebral** NAD+ (measured by 31P-MRS) in Parkinson's patients, confirming central target engagement. NR-SAFE (PMID 38016950): 3000 mg/day raised NAD+/NADP+ up to ~5-fold.

**Cognitive/brain benefit — NOT DEMONSTRATED:**
- Wu 2025 (PMID 39817194): crossover, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled; older adults with subjective cognitive decline / MCI; NR 1 g/day for 8 weeks. **No significant between-group difference on cognition (RBANS, Lumosity).** A within-subject reduction in plasma pTau217 was observed (exploratory biomarker, not a cognitive outcome).
- Long-COVID RCT (PMID 41357333): double-blind, placebo-controlled; NR 2000 mg/day; NAD+ rose 2.6-3.1 fold, but **no significant between-group difference in cognition (ECog, RBANS, TMT-B), fatigue, sleep, anxiety, or depression.**
- NADPARK/NR-SAFE noted exploratory motor (MDS-UPDRS) changes in Parkinson's, but these are small phase-I signals in a disease population, not cognition endpoints, and require confirmation.

**Net:** Robust biomarker (NAD+ up, including in brain) but the cognition RCTs that exist are null. Hence evidence_overall = 2, mito evidence = 2 (driven by the proven biomarker with confirmed central engagement, but cognitive read-through absent), ntrophic = 1 (preclinical only).

## Safety & interactions

NR is generally well tolerated short-term. Across RCTs up to 1000-2000 mg/day for 8-12 weeks (and 3000 mg/day for ~4 weeks in NR-SAFE, PMID 38016950), adverse-event rates were similar to placebo, with no serious adverse events and — unlike nicotinic acid — **no flushing** (low GPR109A affinity). Conze 2019 (PMID 31278280) found no LDL elevation, no homocysteine rise, and no disruption of 1-carbon metabolism at tested doses.

- **Contraindications:** none established.
- **Interactions:** none well-documented in humans.
- **Notable risks:** long-term (>12 weeks / chronic) safety is unknown; high-dose rodent studies reported organ toxicity (liver/kidney/gonads) at exposures far above human dosing, with unclear human relevance.

## Open questions

- Does proven NAD+ elevation (including confirmed cerebral NAD+ rise) translate into any cognitive benefit in healthy adults? Current RCTs say no.
- Is there a population (e.g. deficient, aged, metabolically impaired, or neurodegenerative) where NR yields a real cognitive/functional endpoint, versus only a biomarker shift?
- What is the long-term safety profile beyond a few months?
- Is the exploratory pTau217 reduction (PMID 39817194) reproducible and clinically meaningful, or noise?
- How do brain NAD+ pharmacodynamics relate (if at all) to functional outcomes?
