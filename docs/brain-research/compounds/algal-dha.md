## Summary

Algal DHA is docosahexaenoic acid (DHA) sourced from microalgae (commonly *Schizochytrium* sp.; commercial DHASCO oils), a plant-based, fish-free alternative for raising DHA status. Multiple randomized bioequivalence trials show algal-oil DHA raises plasma phospholipid and erythrocyte DHA comparably to fish oil and to cooked salmon (Arterburn 2008, PMID 18589030; Arterburn 2007, PMID 17713804). Its main practical value is for vegans, vegetarians, and others with low baseline DHA status, where it efficiently raises the Omega-3 Index (Geppert 2005, PMID 16296399).

The brain/cognition rationale is **not algal-specific** — it rides entirely on the broader DHA/omega-3 evidence base (see cross-reference: `omega-3-epa-dha`; **do not double-count** the two entries). DHA is a structural membrane lipid concentrated in synaptic membranes, and that is the mechanistic basis for any nootropic claim. However, cognition-prevention trials in already-replete adults are null (Cochrane, Sydenham 2012, PMID 22696350), with any benefit concentrating in low-DHA-status individuals. Graded modestly (**evidence_overall: 2**): strong, well-replicated evidence for raising DHA status equivalently to fish oil, but weak/conditional evidence for cognitive benefit.

## Mechanism

- **Structural / neurotrophic (ntrophic):** DHA is the dominant n-3 long-chain PUFA in the brain, constituting a large fraction of synaptic membrane phospholipids (notably phosphatidylethanolamine and phosphatidylserine). Its long, polyunsaturated acyl chain confers membrane fluidity and supports neuronal phosphatidylserine synthesis, synaptogenesis, long-term potentiation, and synaptic plasticity (Sun 2022, PMID 35563025). Algal DHA delivers this same substrate; bioequivalence trials confirm it incorporates into erythrocyte and plasma membranes equivalently to fish-derived DHA (PMID 18589030, PMID 17713804).
- **Inflammation/resolution (inflam):** DHA is a precursor for specialized pro-resolving mediators (D-series resolvins, protectins) and remodels membrane fatty-acid composition toward a less pro-inflammatory profile. This is a general DHA mechanism (read-across from the omega-3 entry), modest and status-dependent in humans — hence graded modulate, evidence 2.

Both channels are inherited from the DHA/omega-3 base. The source (algal vs fish) changes only the supply route and status caveats, not the downstream neurobiology.

## Evidence

**Bioequivalence (strong, randomized).** Arterburn 2008 (PMID 18589030) randomized 32 healthy adults to 600 mg DHA/day from algal-oil capsules vs cooked salmon for 2 weeks; changes in plasma phospholipid and erythrocyte DHA were similar between groups — "nutritionally equivalent." Arterburn 2007 (PMID 17713804) showed dose-dependent, linear DHA increases in plasma phospholipids and erythrocytes at 200/600/1000 mg DHA/day from two algal oils and a DHA-fortified food, all bioequivalent and safe. These establish that algal DHA is a genuine substitute for fish oil for raising DHA status.

**Vegan/vegetarian DHA status (low-status benefit).** Geppert 2005 (PMID 16296399), a double-blind RCT in 104 vegetarians, found 0.94 g/day microalgal DHA for 8 weeks raised the Omega-3 Index from 4.8% to 8.4%, with 69% of supplemented subjects (vs 0% placebo) reaching the desirable >8% threshold. García-Maldonado 2023 (PMID 36418565), a crossover RCT across omnivores, lacto-ovo vegetarians, and vegans, confirmed algal DHA significantly raises blood DHA across diet groups — i.e., the bioavailability advantage is most consequential where baseline status is low.

**Cognition (null in replete; cross-reference omega-3).** The Cochrane review (Sydenham 2012, PMID 22696350) found no benefit of omega-3 PUFA supplementation on cognitive function in cognitively healthy older people over the studied durations. This null replete-adult signal is the central honesty caveat: algal DHA reliably fixes DHA *status*, but fixing status in already-replete people has not translated into cognitive gains. Any plausible benefit concentrates in low-status individuals (vegans/vegetarians, low fish intake) where the status deficit is correctable. **Cognitive efficacy data are graded via the omega-3 read-across, not from algal-specific cognition trials — do not double-count with `omega-3-epa-dha`.**

## Safety & interactions

- **Generally safe and well tolerated.** Bioequivalence trials reported algal DHA as a safe DHA source (PMID 17713804). The most common adverse effects are mild GI upset and occasional marine-tasting eructation (typically milder than fish oil).
- **Anticoagulants/antiplatelets:** theoretical additive effect on platelet function/bleeding time at higher doses; in practice mild and clinically minor at typical DHA intakes. Use clinical judgment for patients on warfarin or dual antiplatelet therapy.
- **No fish/marine-animal content:** suitable for vegans/vegetarians and avoids methylmercury and other marine contaminant concerns associated with some fish-oil products.
- Contraindicated only for known hypersensitivity to the specific algal oil/formulation.

## Open questions

- Whether DHA-only algal oils (low EPA) match mixed EPA+DHA fish oil for any non-structural (e.g., mood, inflammation) endpoints. (unsourced)
- Optimal maintenance dose to keep the Omega-3 Index in target range specifically in plant-based diets without fish-derived EPA.
