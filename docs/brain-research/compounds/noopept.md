## Summary

Noopept (N-phenylacetyl-L-prolylglycine ethyl ester; GVS-111; INN **omberacetam**)
is a proline-containing dipeptide nootropic developed at the Zakusov Research
Institute of Pharmacology (Russia) as a low-dose analogue of piracetam. **It is a
prescription drug in Russia but is NOT approved by the FDA**; outside Russia it is
sold as an unapproved research chemical / supplement, so purity and identity are not
guaranteed (**research-grade — flag**).

The mechanistic story most cited for Noopept — upregulation of the neurotrophins
**NGF and BDNF** — comes from **rodent (preclinical) studies only**. The human
evidence base is **small, mostly Russian, predominantly open-label, and confined to
impaired populations** (mild cognitive impairment of vascular/traumatic origin,
post-stroke), raising quality-, access-, and bias-related concerns. **Randomized
controlled-trial evidence of cognitive benefit in healthy adults is essentially
absent.** Overall evidence is graded **LOW (2)** and should be read with heavy
skepticism.

## Mechanism

- **Neurotrophic (ntrophic):** In rats, Noopept increased hippocampal mRNA/expression
  of both **NGF and BDNF** after acute administration; chronic dosing did not produce
  tolerance and even potentiated the neurotrophic effect, with raised BDNF mRNA in
  hippocampus and hypothalamus (Ostrovskaya et al. 2008, PMID: 19240853; mechanistic
  follow-up Vakhitova et al. 2010, PMID: 21395007). **These are rodent findings;**
  human neurotrophin data are not available. Per grading rules, preclinical BDNF/NGF
  upregulation is capped at evidence 2.
- **Glutamatergic (glu):** Noopept is described as a prodrug generating the endogenous
  dipeptide **cycloprolylglycine**, proposed to modulate **AMPA receptors**. Direct
  radioligand binding shows only weak interaction at AMPA sites (IC50 ~80 microM,
  roughly an order of magnitude weaker than nooglutil) (PMID: 21476267). Preclinical;
  the functional relevance in humans is unestablished.
- **Anti-inflammatory / antioxidant (inflam):** In cellular and rodent neurodegeneration
  models, Noopept attenuated amyloid-beta-induced apoptosis, oxidative stress, calcium
  overload, and tau hyperphosphorylation, and restored neurite outgrowth (Ostrovskaya
  et al. 2014, PMID: 25096780). Preclinical only.

## Evidence

**Impaired populations (small, mostly Russian, mostly open-label):**
- **Mild cognitive disorders of vascular/traumatic origin:** A Russian comparative
  study of Noopept (10 mg twice daily) versus piracetam over ~56 days in patients with
  mild cognitive disorders reported improvement in cognition and mood; in the Noopept
  group MMSE rose (reported ~26 to ~29). Adverse effects in a minority included sleep
  disturbance, irritability, and increased blood pressure (Neznamov & Teleshova 2009,
  PMID: 19234797). **Open-label / comparative design, single research group, small
  sample — high risk of bias and limited generalizability.**
- **Post-stroke / vascular cognitive impairment:** Additional Russian reports describe
  cognitive improvement with Noopept in post-stroke patients, but primary peer-reviewed
  PMIDs were not independently verified in this review — **mark as (unsourced)** pending
  confirmation.

**Healthy adults:**
- **No randomized controlled-trial evidence of cognitive enhancement in healthy adults
  was identified (absent / unsourced).** All located human evidence is in impaired
  populations.

**Gaps (unsourced):** No modern, adequately powered, independent, placebo-controlled
RCTs; no validated healthy-adult dosing or dose-response; no robust human
pharmacokinetics (half-life); no long-term human safety data; the BDNF/NGF mechanism
has not been demonstrated in humans.

## Safety & interactions

- **Regulatory / legal:** **Prescription drug in Russia** (omberacetam); **not
  FDA-approved** and sold as an **unapproved research chemical / supplement** elsewhere,
  with no guarantee of purity or identity (**research-grade — flag**).
- **Contraindications (precautionary, unsourced):** pregnancy/lactation (no data);
  severe hepatic/renal impairment (not studied); known hypersensitivity.
- **Interactions (mechanistic/observational inference, unsourced):** theoretical
  relevance with antihypertensive drugs given the blood-pressure increase seen in some
  trial patients; no controlled human interaction data with other nootropics, cholinergics,
  or CNS-active drugs.
- **Notable risks:** trial-reported sleep disturbance, irritability, and increased blood
  pressure in a minority of impaired patients (open-label data, PMID: 19234797); unknown
  long-term safety in healthy adults; small, mostly Russian, open-label evidence base.

## Open questions

- Do the rodent BDNF/NGF effects translate to any measurable neurotrophic or cognitive
  change in humans?
- Are the Russian MCI/stroke benefits reproducible in modern, independent,
  placebo-controlled RCTs outside Russia?
- Is there *any* validated benefit in healthy adults, or is all human evidence confined
  to impaired/vascular/traumatic populations?
- What is the correct, evidence-based dose, human pharmacokinetics, and half-life?
- What is the long-term safety profile, and how meaningful is the blood-pressure signal?
- Purity/identity and legal-status concerns of research-grade material sold outside Russia.
- Confirm primary PMIDs/DOIs for the post-stroke/vascular reports (currently unsourced).
