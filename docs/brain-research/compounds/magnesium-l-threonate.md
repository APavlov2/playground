## Summary

Magnesium L-threonate (Magtein / MgT / MMFS-01) is a chelated magnesium salt marketed for cognition on the premise that it raises *brain* magnesium more effectively than other salts and thereby increases synaptic density. That premise rests primarily on a single influential **preclinical** study in rats (Slutsky 2010, PMID 20152124). Human evidence is **few, small, and frequently industry-linked**: the main cognition RCT (Liu 2016, PMID 26519439, n~44) was authored by someone affiliated with the manufacturer (Neurocentria), and a later positive combination-formula RCT (Zhang 2022, PMID 36558392) was manufacturer-funded and did not test threonate in isolation.

This entry deliberately separates two very different claims:
1. The **threonate-specific synaptic / cognition claim** (raises brain Mg -> more synapses -> better memory). This is graded **modestly (overall 2)** because it is preclinical-dominant with only small, industry-linked human pilots.
2. **Magnesium deficiency correction**, which has broad, well-supported relevance. Low magnesium status is associated epidemiologically with depression and poorer sleep, and supplementation helps in deficient/depressed populations — but this is general magnesium physiology and does **not** validate the threonate-specific synaptic-density story.

**Overall evidence grade: 2** (preclinical-dominant for the headline claim; human data small and conflicted).

## Mechanism

The proposed mechanism (from rodent work) is that L-threonate facilitates magnesium delivery to the CNS, elevating cerebrospinal-fluid / brain magnesium. Elevated neuronal magnesium is reported to upregulate NR2B-containing NMDA receptors and downstream plasticity signaling, increase the density of functional presynaptic release sites while lowering release probability, and raise hippocampal synaptophysin/synaptobrevin-positive puncta — interpreted as increased synaptic density and enhanced LTP (Slutsky 2010, PMID 20152124).

Important caveat: the magnitude of the brain-magnesium advantage of the threonate salt over cheaper salts, and whether the rodent synaptic-density effect occurs in humans, are **not** established in humans. The "synapse density enhancer" framing is largely a marketing translation of preclinical findings.

Separately, magnesium is a physiological NMDA-receptor pore blocker and a cofactor in hundreds of reactions; correcting deficiency plausibly normalizes neuronal excitability, HPA/stress responses, and sleep — but these are general-magnesium effects, not threonate-specific.

## Evidence

**Preclinical (foundational, rats) — Slutsky 2010, PMID 20152124 / DOI 10.1016/j.neuron.2009.12.026.** Showed that MgT raised brain magnesium and enhanced learning, working memory, and short/long-term memory in young and aged rats, with corresponding hippocampal synaptic-density and NR2B/LTP changes. This is the single most-cited support for the compound and is *animal data only*.

**Human pilot RCT — Liu 2016, PMID 26519439 / DOI 10.3233/JAD-150538.** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of MMFS-01 (magnesium L-threonate) at ~1.5-2 g/day for 12 weeks in ~44 older adults with cognitive complaints. Reported improvement in a composite cognitive measure and Trail Making Test-B speed vs placebo. Limitations: small sample, single trial, and **industry linkage** — an author was affiliated with Neurocentria, Inc., the developer/commercializer of MMFS-01. Treat as hypothesis-generating, not confirmatory.

**Manufacturer-funded combination-formula RCT — Zhang 2022, PMID 36558392 / DOI 10.3390/nu14245235.** Double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in healthy Chinese adults of a Magtein-*based* formula (combined with other ingredients, e.g. phosphatidylserine), reporting cognitive improvement. Funded by the manufacturer and not a clean test of threonate alone; weak support for the isolated-threonate claim.

**Deficiency / status epidemiology and supplementation (distinct from the threonate claim):**
- Depression: a systematic review/meta-analysis of RCTs found magnesium supplementation reduced depression scores in adults with depressive disorder (Moabedi 2023, PMID 38213402 / DOI 10.3389/fpsyt.2023.1333261). This concerns magnesium generally and deficiency states, not Magtein.
- Sleep: dietary magnesium intake was associated with longer sleep duration and a borderline association with better sleep quality in the CARDIA cohort (Zhang 2022 Sleep, PMID 34883514 / DOI 10.1093/sleep/zsab276). Observational; dietary magnesium, not threonate.

**Bottom line:** robust *animal* mechanism + thin, conflicted *human* cognition data + separate, broader evidence for magnesium *deficiency correction*. Grade 2.

## Safety & interactions

- **Renal impairment:** the key contraindication. Magnesium is renally cleared; in CKD/renal failure, supplemental magnesium can accumulate and cause hypermagnesemia (lethargy, hypotension, bradycardia, in severe cases respiratory/cardiac depression). Use only with clinical monitoring.
- **GI / laxative effect:** loose stools, diarrhea, nausea, and cramping are the most common dose-dependent adverse effects of oral magnesium salts.
- **Antibiotic and drug chelation:** magnesium chelates oral tetracyclines and fluoroquinolones (and reduces bisphosphonate absorption); separate administration by ~2-4 hours.
- **Additive risk:** potassium-sparing diuretics or reduced renal function increase hypermagnesemia risk.
- Elemental magnesium delivered by typical threonate dosing (~108-144 mg/day) is modest relative to the RDA, which lowers (but does not eliminate) systemic-overload concern in people with normal kidneys.

## Open questions

- Does magnesium L-threonate actually raise human brain/CSF magnesium more than cheaper salts, and is any such difference clinically meaningful? (Largely untested in humans.)
- Are the rodent synaptic-density / NR2B findings reproducible in humans? No direct human synaptic-density evidence exists. (unsourced)
- Do independent, adequately powered, non-industry-funded RCTs replicate the Liu 2016 cognitive benefit? Current human data are small and conflicted by funding/affiliation.
- How much of any benefit is simply **deficiency correction** versus a threonate-specific synaptic effect? Trials in magnesium-replete vs deficient participants are needed to disentangle this.
- Long-term safety and efficacy beyond 12 weeks are uncharacterized.
