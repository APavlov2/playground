## Summary

Pterostilbene is a dimethylated analog of resveratrol with notably better oral bioavailability than the parent compound (greater lipophilicity and metabolic stability). Despite frequent marketing as a cognitive/longevity agent, **human brain or cognition evidence is essentially absent**. The only human RCT (Riche 2014) measured blood pressure and lipids — not cognition — and is not a neuro study. All neuro-relevant data are **preclinical** (rodent): improvements in memory tasks, antioxidant and anti-neuroinflammatory effects, and synaptic/neurotrophic markers. Brain claims are graded LOW (overall 1). Direct human cognitive efficacy: **(unsourced)**.

## Mechanism

Reported preclinical mechanisms center on antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activity. In rodent models pterostilbene engages SIRT1/Nrf2-linked antioxidant signaling, suppresses mitochondria-dependent apoptosis, and inhibits microglial ROS/NF-kB inflammatory signaling with reduced inflammatory cytokine expression (PMID:32490497). Neurotrophic/synaptic effects (BDNF modulation, increased dentate-gyrus synaptic markers) are described in aged and lesion models (PMID:30816671). These mechanisms are derived entirely from animal and in vitro work; human mechanistic confirmation in brain is **(unsourced)**.

## Evidence

**Preclinical neuro (rodent):**
- Streptozotocin (ICV) memory-decline rats: oral pterostilbene (10, 30, 50 mg/kg) improved Morris water maze and novel object recognition performance, enhanced antioxidant defenses, improved ATPase activity, and decreased inflammatory gene expression (PMID:28174611).
- Aged rats with mild cognitive decline: chronic pterostilbene improved behavioral/memory-consolidation performance and increased dentate-gyrus synaptic markers (PMID:30816671).
- Cerebral ischemia-reperfusion rats: pterostilbene reduced infarct volume and edema and promoted neuronal survival via suppression of microglial ROS/NF-kB signaling (PMID:32490497).

**Human:**
- Riche 2014 RCT (n=80, hyperlipidemic adults): **blood pressure and lipids only — NOT cognition.** High-dose pterostilbene (125 mg twice daily) lowered systolic (-7.8 mmHg) and diastolic (-7.3 mmHg) blood pressure; monotherapy raised LDL (+17.1 mg/dL), an effect not seen with grape-extract combination (PMID:25057276, DOI:10.1155/2014/459165).

**Brain / cognition human data:** No randomized or controlled human trial has assessed pterostilbene on any cognitive outcome — **(unsourced)**.

## Safety & interactions

Human safety data are limited and derive mainly from the single 6-8 week metabolic RCT. The notable signal is a **LDL cholesterol increase with pterostilbene monotherapy** (+17.1 mg/dL; Riche 2014), which was attenuated/absent when combined with grape extract (P=0.47) — suggesting a possible mitigating interaction, though data are sparse and the mechanism is unconfirmed. A blood-pressure-lowering effect at high dose is documented; interaction with antihypertensive medication is untested. No contraindications are established from controlled human data; long-term safety is **(unsourced)**.

## Open questions

- Is there any human cognitive benefit at all? No human cognition trial exists.
- What is human brain bioavailability / CNS exposure? Human PK in brain is unknown.
- Is the monotherapy LDL rise clinically meaningful with chronic use, and does grape-extract co-administration reliably prevent it?
- Do rodent SIRT1/Nrf2 and anti-neuroinflammatory mechanisms translate to humans at tolerable oral doses?
- Optimal dose/onset for any neuro endpoint is undefined — current human dosing comes only from a metabolic trial.
