## Summary

Phenylpiracetam (phenotropil, fonturacetam, Actitropil) is a phenylated piracetam analog developed and marketed primarily in Russia as a prescription (Rx) nootropic. Unlike most racetams it has clear psychostimulant properties and is the **only racetam on the WADA Prohibited List** (in-competition banned stimulant) — this must be flagged prominently for any athlete.

**Evidence is graded LOW (overall 2).** The human clinical literature is almost entirely **Russian-language, small, and conducted in patient populations** (ischemic stroke rehabilitation, vascular/organic encephalopathy, asthenia), not in healthy adults. **Healthy-adult RCT evidence for cognitive enhancement is essentially absent.** Russian trials carry meaningful access and quality concerns (language barrier to peer review, small/older studies, limited methodological transparency). The mechanistic data implicating dopamine/norepinephrine reuptake inhibition are **preclinical (rodent)** and enantiomer-specific; the marketed racemic drug's human mechanism is not directly characterized.

## Mechanism

- **Dopamine / norepinephrine (da, ne) — preclinical:** Enantiomer-dependent monoamine reuptake inhibition. The (S)-enantiomer is a selective dopamine transporter (DAT) inhibitor (PMID 28743458); the (R)-enantiomer behaves as a dual norepinephrine-dopamine reuptake inhibitor (NDRI) with ~11-fold lower NET than DAT affinity, and also shows neuroprotective/anti-inflammatory activity in rodent inflammation models (PMID 32279140). This DAT/NET activity is consistent with the drug's observed psychostimulant and antiasthenic (anti-fatigue) profile, but all of it derives from rodent work — human target engagement is **(unsourced)**.
- **Glutamate / AMPA (glu) — preclinical/inferred:** AMPA receptor potentiation is a frequently cited racetam class effect, but phenylpiracetam-specific human glutamatergic data are absent; graded 1 (modulate).
- **Other receptor activity (e.g., nicotinic ACh binding) is reported in secondary/encyclopedic sources but not robustly established in primary human data — (unsourced).**

Pharmacokinetics: oral bioavailability ~100%, onset <1 hour, elimination half-life ~3-5 hours (human).

## Evidence

**Patient populations (Russian trials — small, mostly Russian-language):**

- **Stroke rehabilitation:** A trial of 400 ischemic-stroke patients (200 treated with 3 courses of phenotropil 400 mg/day over the year after stroke vs 200 controls) reported significantly better restoration of neurologic function and activities of daily living in the treated group (p < 0.0001) (PMID 21626817). Single-center, open design typical of this literature.
- **Encephalopathy / organic brain lesions:** 99 patients with encephalopathy following cerebrovascular events, head trauma, or glioma surgery; phenotropil 200 mg/day for one month improved movement disturbances, coordination, memory, and attention, with EEG normalization trends (PMID 16447562). Vascular encephalopathy was also examined as a comparative study (PMID 16608112; **no abstract available** — results not independently verifiable here).
- **Asthenia:** A 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis (11 studies, 549 patients, mean age 45.9) of fonturacetam 200 mg/day reported a mean MFI-20 asthenia reduction of 16.3 points (95% CI 8.85-23.85; p < 0.0001) at 30 days, with adverse events ~5.5% (transient) (PMID 40047835). Note: the included primary studies are predominantly small Russian trials, so the meta-analysis inherits their quality/publication-bias limitations.

**Healthy adults:** No identifiable peer-reviewed RCT demonstrating cognitive enhancement in healthy adults — **(unsourced / evidence absent).** Claims of memory/focus benefit in healthy users are anecdotal.

**Gaps:** Long-term safety, tolerance kinetics, optimal dosing, and any healthy-population cognitive benefit are all **(unsourced).**

## Safety & interactions

- **WADA ban (critical for athletes):** In-competition banned stimulant; the only racetam on the Prohibited List. Use carries sanction risk.
- **Psychostimulant effects:** Given DAT/NET reuptake inhibition (preclinical) and clinical stimulant profile, expect potential insomnia, agitation, irritability, and increased blood pressure/heart rate. Avoid in uncontrolled hypertension/arrhythmia (formal contraindication labeling **(unsourced)**).
- **Tolerance:** Anecdotally reported to develop to the stimulant/antiasthenic effect with continuous daily use; controlled tolerance data **(unsourced).**
- **Interactions:** Theoretical additive effects with other psychostimulants, dopaminergic/noradrenergic drugs, MAO inhibitors, and sympathomimetics (mechanistic inference; clinical interaction data **(unsourced)**).
- **Legal/regulatory:** Russian Rx and S4 (prescription-only) in Australia; **not FDA-approved** — sale in US supplements/food/medicine is unlawful. Not DEA-scheduled (as of 2016). Treat as **research-grade**.
- **Reported AE rate** in the asthenia meta-analysis was ~5.5%, transient (PMID 40047835) — but this reflects monitored Russian patient trials, not healthy chronic users.

## Open questions

- Does phenylpiracetam produce any reproducible cognitive benefit in **healthy adults**, or are observed effects confined to patient/asthenic populations? (currently **unsourced**)
- What is the human mechanism of the marketed **racemic** drug, given enantiomer-divergent DAT/NET profiles seen only in rodents?
- Tolerance and dependence liability with chronic use — kinetics and reversibility? (**unsourced**)
- Long-term cardiovascular and safety profile in non-clinical users? (**unsourced**)
- To what extent is the Russian-trial evidence base affected by publication bias, lack of blinding, and limited independent replication?
