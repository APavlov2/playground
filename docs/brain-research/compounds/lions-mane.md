## Summary
Lion's Mane (Hericium erinaceus) is a culinary/medicinal mushroom marketed as a neurotrophic nootropic. Its appeal rests almost entirely on a well-characterized **preclinical** mechanism: its terpenoid constituents (hericenones from the fruiting body, erinacines from the mycelium) stimulate nerve growth factor (NGF) synthesis in cell and animal models. The **human** evidence is thin: a handful of small (n=30–41), short (4–16 week) RCTs with mixed and largely unreplicated results. Overall evidence is graded **2** — robust mechanism, weak clinical confirmation. Honest reading: promising but unproven for brain optimization in healthy adults.

## Mechanism
Hericenones (A–H) and erinacines (A–C, H, I) induce NGF biosynthesis **in vitro**, but notably do so mainly in glial cells rather than neurons directly, and at concentrations achieved in cell culture rather than demonstrated in human brain (Lai 2013 — PMID:24266378; foundational mechanism reviewed in Mori/Kawagishi-lineage literature). In rodents these compounds increase hippocampal NGF and neurite outgrowth and improve learning tasks.

Two open questions dominate the human translation and should not be glossed over:
1. **BBB penetration at supplement doses.** Some sources assert erinacines/hericenones cross the blood–brain barrier by passive diffusion, but this is largely inferred from animal/structural data; there is no human pharmacokinetic confirmation that oral supplement doses produce CNS-relevant concentrations. Treat "raises NGF in the human brain" as an **open question, not an established fact**.
2. **NGF causality in humans.** No human study has shown that Lion's Mane raises central NGF. The one human neurotrophin signal (raised serum BDNF/NPY in La Monica 2024 — DOI:10.1016/j.jff.2024.106120) is peripheral, indirect, and unreplicated.

The serotonergic/mood and anti-inflammatory channels are even more speculative — supported by animal and in-vitro work plus one tiny mood trial, with no direct human mechanistic demonstration.

## Evidence
The human RCT base is small and should be read conservatively:

- **Mori 2009 (impaired) — PMID:18844328.** Double-blind placebo-controlled trial in older Japanese adults (50–80 y) with mild cognitive impairment; n=30 (15/15), 3000 mg/day fruiting-body powder for 16 weeks. The Lion's Mane group improved on the Hasegawa Dementia Scale-Revised (HDS-R) versus placebo, but scores **declined after a 4-week washout**, suggesting no durable effect. Very small n; single center; cognition-scale (not biomarker) endpoint.
- **Nagano 2010 (menopausal mood) — PMID:20834180.** n=30 women, HE-containing cookies vs placebo, 4 weeks. Reductions in CES-D depression and anxiety/"indefinite complaints" measures. Tiny, short, food-vehicle delivery, subjective endpoints.
- **Docherty 2023 (healthy young adults) — PMID:38004235.** n=41, 1.8 g/day; the much-cited "acute speed-of-performance" finding is a faster Stroop time at 60 min post-dose (p=0.005) on a **single task**, with otherwise null/mixed results (and reduced immediate word-recall accuracy acutely); chronic 28-day arm showed only a non-significant trend toward reduced stress (p=0.051). Authors themselves urge caution given the small sample.
- **La Monica 2024 (healthy adults) — DOI:10.1016/j.jff.2024.106120.** n=33, erinacine A-enriched HE, 8 weeks; task-specific cognitive improvement emerging only after covariate adjustment, plus raised serum BDNF/NPY. Pilot-grade; not PubMed-indexed (Journal of Functional Foods).

**Replication gaps:** different preparations (fruiting body vs erinacine-enriched mycelium), different populations (MCI vs menopausal vs healthy young), different endpoints, and no adequately powered confirmatory trial. The 2025 acute literature is itself mixed (some null composite outcomes), reinforcing the cautious grade.

## Safety & interactions (research metadata)
Generally well tolerated in the short trials above, with no serious adverse events reported at studied doses. Rare hypersensitivity reactions exist in the case-report literature (acute respiratory distress and dermatologic/skin reactions attributed to Hericium exposure) — verify relevance per patient, especially in those with mushroom allergy. Anticoagulant/antiplatelet interaction is sometimes asserted but is theoretical and **(unsourced)** at the level of controlled human data. Long-term safety beyond ~16 weeks is uncharacterized.

## Open questions
- **BBB penetration:** Do oral supplement doses of hericenones/erinacines reach CNS-relevant concentrations in humans? Unresolved.
- **NGF causality in humans:** No human study links supplementation to central NGF change; peripheral BDNF/NPY signals are indirect.
- **Durability:** Mori 2009's washout decline suggests benefit may not persist — needs longer trials.
- **Replication & standardization:** Heterogeneous preparations and endpoints; an adequately powered, biomarker-anchored confirmatory RCT in both healthy and impaired populations is missing.
