## Summary

Piracetam is the original racetam (a cyclic GABA derivative) and the prototype "nootropic," used as a prescription metabolic-cerebral enhancer (Nootropil/Nootropyl) in many countries for cortical myoclonus and selected cognitive/vertigo indications. It is NOT an approved US dietary supplement. Its proposed actions are AMPA-type glutamate modulation, hemorheologic/microvascular improvement, and restoration of membrane fluidity supporting mitochondrial function. Human cognitive-enhancement evidence is weak: the Cochrane review found evidence insufficient to support clinical use in dementia or cognitive impairment, and healthy-adult enhancement data are sparse and dated. Cognition grade here is LOW (evidence_overall 2).

**REGULATORY / RESEARCH-GRADE FLAG (prominent):** Piracetam is a prescription medicine in numerous jurisdictions and is not approved as a US dietary supplement. Legal status varies by country. It requires renal dose adjustment and carries an antiplatelet effect.

## Mechanism

- **Glutamate / AMPA (glu, modulate; preclinical):** Piracetam is described as a positive allosteric modulator of AMPA-type glutamate receptors, facilitating glutamatergic transmission and long-term potentiation. It has low or no defined affinity for a specific receptor, and much of the effect is attributed to membrane-level interaction rather than classical receptor binding. Evidence is predominantly preclinical (PMID 16007238).
- **Cerebral blood flow / hemorheology (cbf, up; impaired):** At the vascular level piracetam reduces erythrocyte adhesion to endothelium, decreases platelet aggregation, improves red-cell deformability, and facilitates microcirculation while hindering vasospasm. This rheological profile underlies its use in vascular/microcirculatory contexts (PMID 16007238).
- **Mitochondria / membrane fluidity (mito, up; preclinical):** Piracetam restores neuronal membrane fluidity selectively in aged or oxidatively-stressed membranes (little effect in young membranes), which enhances mobility of respiratory-chain complexes and improves mitochondrial membrane potential and ATP production, with reduced caspase-9 activation (PMID 16284628; PMID 20877425). This is a cell/animal-model mechanism, not a demonstrated driver of cognition in healthy humans.

Direct effects on other classical channels (ach, da, ser, gaba, ne, ntrophic, inflam, glymph, hpa) are not well-established as primary cognitive mechanisms — (unsourced) — and are omitted from channels.

## Evidence

- **Cochrane (dementia / cognitive impairment) - INSUFFICIENT:** Flicker & Grimley Evans concluded that the published evidence does not support the use of piracetam in dementia or cognitive impairment; although a statistically significant effect appeared on global impression of change, no benefit was shown on more specific cognitive measures, and the authors judged the evidence inadequate for clinical use though sufficient to justify further research (PMID 11405971; DOI 10.1002/14651858.CD001011). This is the anchor for the conservative grade and the primary honesty caveat.
- **Healthy adults - weak/dated:** The most-cited healthy-volunteer study (Dimond & Brouwers, 1976) reported increased verbal learning after 14 days (not 7) of piracetam in normal subjects (PMID 826948; DOI 10.1007/BF00426834). It is small, old, and not replicated by a robust modern RCT base; healthy-adult cognitive-enhancement evidence is essentially weak-to-absent and should not be over-read.
- **Mechanistic reviews:** Winblad's pharmacological review summarizes membrane-fluidity, glutamatergic/cholinergic, neuroprotective, and microvascular mechanisms and clinical use in cognitive disorders, vertigo, myoclonus, dyslexia, and sickle-cell anemia (PMID 16007238). Mitochondrial/membrane-fluidity mechanisms are detailed in preclinical work (PMID 16284628; PMID 20877425).
- **Established Rx indication (non-cognitive):** Cortical myoclonus is the most robustly supported clinical use; this is a motor, not a cognitive-enhancement, indication.

Net: glu graded 2 (mechanistically described but largely preclinical), cbf graded 2 (plausible hemorheologic mechanism with impaired-population rationale, weak trials), mito graded 2 (preclinical membrane-fluidity/ATP data). Overall cognition grade 2 (LOW), driven down by the Cochrane "insufficient" finding and the thin healthy-adult base.

## Safety & interactions (research metadata)

- **Renal dosing (important):** Piracetam is 80-100% renally excreted unchanged; clearance correlates with creatinine clearance. Daily dose must be reduced according to CrCl (commonly ~50% reduction at CrCl 30-59, ~30% at CrCl 15-30); severe impairment / ESRD (CrCl <20 mL/min) is a labeling contraindication. Half-life rises markedly in the elderly and in renal failure.
- **Bleeding / antiplatelet:** Piracetam reduces platelet aggregation and improves red-cell deformability; additive risk with anticoagulants/antiplatelets (warfarin, aspirin, clopidogrel). Caution around surgery and in patients with bleeding tendency; cerebral hemorrhage is a contraindication.
- **Legal / regulatory status:** Research-grade. Prescription medicine (Nootropil/Nootropyl) in many countries for myoclonus and selected cognitive/vertigo indications; NOT an FDA-approved US dietary supplement. Availability and legality vary by jurisdiction.
- **Other contraindications:** Huntington's chorea, hypersensitivity to piracetam/pyrrolidone derivatives.
- **Interactions:** Thyroid hormone (case reports of confusion/irritability/sleep disturbance); theoretical additive CNS effects with stimulants/other nootropics.
- **Common AEs:** Nervousness, agitation, insomnia, hyperkinesia, somnolence, weight gain, GI upset — generally dose-related.

This is research metadata, not medical advice.

## Open questions

- Does piracetam produce any clinically meaningful cognitive benefit under adequately powered modern RCTs, or is the Cochrane "insufficient" verdict effectively definitive for dementia/cognitive impairment?
- Are the AMPA-modulation and membrane-fluidity/mitochondrial mechanisms translatable to durable cognitive endpoints in humans, or do they remain preclinical curiosities?
- Is there any reproducible healthy-adult enhancement signal beyond the 1976 Dimond data?
- How relevant is the hemorheologic/microvascular mechanism to cognition versus its better-supported roles in vascular and sickle-cell contexts?
- What is the real-world exposure/quality profile given non-standardized gray-market supply in jurisdictions where it is not an approved supplement?
