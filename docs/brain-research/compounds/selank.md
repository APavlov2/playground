## Summary

Selank is a synthetic heptapeptide (Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg-Pro-Gly-Pro), an analog of the
endogenous immunopeptide tuftsin, developed in Russia primarily as an **anxiolytic**.
It is administered intranasally and is registered as a prescription drug in Russia.

> **RESEARCH-GRADE / LEGAL STATUS — read first.** Selank is **not approved** by the
> FDA, EMA, or other major Western regulators. It is a **Russian prescription medicine**
> and is **unapproved/experimental** elsewhere. Material sold internationally is
> typically gray-market "research-grade" peptide of uncertain purity and is not a
> regulated pharmaceutical. Treat all benefit claims with caution.

**Evidence honesty.** Nearly the entire evidence base is **Russian and/or preclinical**.
The human clinical data come from a small number of Russian trials (generalized anxiety
disorder, neurasthenia, anxiety-asthenic disorders), and mechanistic claims rest on
rodent and cell-line studies. **Western randomized controlled trial evidence is
essentially absent.** Overall evidence grade is **LOW (2)**.

## Mechanism

Selank derives from tuftsin and retains some immunomodulatory character while showing
anxiolytic and putative nootropic activity. Proposed mechanisms (mostly preclinical):

- **GABAergic modulation.** Selank alters expression of GABA-A receptor subunit and
  GABAergic neurotransmission-related genes in rat frontal cortex and in IMR-32 cells,
  consistent with an indirect/allosteric influence on GABAergic tone rather than direct
  receptor binding (PMID: 26924987; PMID: 28293190).
- **Serotonergic modulation.** Selank enhances 5-HT metabolism in the rat brainstem,
  differing from tuftsin, with region-specific monoamine turnover changes
  (PMID: 19803361).
- **Neurotrophic (BDNF).** Intranasal Selank increases BDNF mRNA and protein in the rat
  hippocampus (PMID: 18841804) and normalizes BDNF in an ethanol-induced memory
  impairment model (PMID: 31625062).
- **Anti-stress / benzodiazepine interaction.** In unpredictable chronic mild stress,
  Selank potentiated the anxiolytic effect of diazepam in rats (PMID: 28280289).

Detailed receptor-level pharmacology, human pharmacokinetics, and a clear HPA-axis
mechanism are **(unsourced)**.

## Evidence

**Human (Russian clinical trials — small, mostly open or active-comparator):**
- In 62 patients with generalized anxiety disorder and neurasthenia, intranasal Selank
  (n=30) was compared with the benzodiazepine medazepam (n=32); anxiolytic effect was
  reported as comparable, with additional anti-asthenic/psychostimulant effects ascribed
  to Selank (PMID: 18454096). Russian-language paper; methodology hard to verify
  independently.
- A comparison of Selank versus phenazepam in anxiety disorders reported comparable
  anxiolytic effect and good tolerability (PMID: 25176261).
- Immunomodulatory effects were reported in patients with anxiety-asthenic disorders
  (PMID: 18577961).

**Preclinical / mechanistic:**
- GABAergic gene-expression changes in rodent cortex and IMR-32 cells (PMID: 26924987;
  PMID: 28293190).
- Serotonin metabolism changes in rat brain (PMID: 19803361).
- BDNF upregulation in rat hippocampus and protection in an ethanol memory model
  (PMID: 18841804; PMID: 31625062).
- Potentiation of diazepam anxiolysis under chronic mild stress (PMID: 28280289).

**Western RCT evidence:** essentially **absent**. No large, blinded, placebo-controlled
Western trials establishing efficacy or safety in healthy or anxious populations were
identified. Cognitive-enhancement ("nootropic") claims in healthy people are
**(unsourced)** by rigorous human data.

## Safety & interactions

Human safety data are **sparse** and derived from small Russian trials, where Selank was
generally described as well tolerated without the sedation, cognitive impairment, or
dependence typical of benzodiazepines — but these are small, mostly non-blinded studies
and **long-term safety is unknown**.

- **Contraindications:** pregnancy/lactation (no adequate data); known hypersensitivity.
- **Interactions:** preclinical potentiation of diazepam's anxiolytic effect
  (PMID: 28280289) suggests possible additive effects with benzodiazepines; theoretical
  additive effects with other GABAergic/serotonergic CNS agents are **(unsourced)**
  clinically.
- **Notable risks:** unregulated research-grade supply (purity/sterility/dose not
  assured); possible local nasal irritation from intranasal use **(unsourced incidence)**;
  immunomodulatory activity of uncertain clinical significance; overall thin human safety
  dataset.

## Open questions

- Does Selank produce reproducible anxiolytic or cognitive benefit in **blinded,
  placebo-controlled Western trials**? (Currently unanswered.)
- What is the **human pharmacokinetic profile** (onset, half-life, CNS penetration of
  intranasal dosing)? **(unsourced)**
- Is the BDNF/neurotrophic effect relevant at human doses, or limited to rodent
  high-dose paradigms?
- What is the true **HPA-axis mechanism** behind the anti-stress effect? **(unsourced)**
- **Long-term safety, dependence potential, and immunomodulatory consequences** in
  chronic human use are uncharacterized.
