## Summary

DMAE (dimethylaminoethanol; deanol) is a legacy "smart drug" / cholinergic
supplement that has been marketed for decades on the claim that it raises brain
acetylcholine. **The human cognitive evidence is old, weak, and largely negative
or equivocal, and the headline mechanism is disputed.** Controlled and open-label
trials in elderly cognition, senile dementia, and tardive dyskinesia mostly
failed to show meaningful, reproducible benefit. There is also a
developmental-toxicity (teratogenicity) signal in animal models. **Graded LOW
(1–2). It should not be presented as an effective cognitive enhancer.**

## Mechanism

The marketed mechanism — DMAE as a dietary precursor that crosses the BBB,
is methylated to choline, and thereby increases acetylcholine synthesis — is
**not supported and is actively contradicted by the better mechanistic data:**

- DMAE is **not reliably methylated to choline in vivo.** A modern PK study
  found no formation of labeled choline in serum after DMAE dosing and concluded
  DMAE was not metabolized to choline (Shipkowski et al., 2019 — PMID:31100288).
- A gas-chromatographic evaluation in rodents detected **no increase in brain
  ACh** in most conditions and explicitly questioned DMAE's "suggested role as
  an immediate precursor of ACh" (Zahniser et al., 1977 — PMID:850128).
- Rather than feeding the cholinergic system, DMAE **competitively inhibits
  high-affinity choline transport across the blood–brain barrier**, with carrier
  affinity at least as high as choline's; it raises blood choline while
  suppressing brain choline uptake — two opposing effects that tend to cancel
  (Millington et al., 1978 — PMID:727735). This predicts a null-to-anticholinergic
  net effect, the opposite of the marketing claim.

**Bottom line:** the "raises acetylcholine" story is disputed/unsupported.
The ACh channel is included only to flag the historical claim, graded **2**
with direction "uncertain."

Cognitive / clinical trials (note populations):

- **Healthy/normal elderly cognition — NEGATIVE.** Deanol 900 mg/day for 21 days
  produced no improvement in word-list learning, reaction time, or digit decoding;
  authors concluded deanol "seems to be an ineffective treatment for the normal
  slowing of cognitive function" in the elderly (Marsh & Linnoila, 1979 —
  PMID:120549). Some evoked-potential amplitude changes occurred but did not
  translate to cognitive benefit.
- **Senile dementia — EQUIVOCAL / mostly negative for cognition.** Small (n=14)
  open-label, uncontrolled 4-week trial: deanol "may not improve memory," with
  only nonspecific behavioral changes (mood/motivation) reported. No controlled
  cognitive benefit; weak, low-quality design (Ferris et al., 1977 — PMID:864168).
- **Tardive dyskinesia (cholinergic test bed) — NEGATIVE / equivocal.** A
  placebo-controlled study (≤1.5 g/day, 3 wk) found deanol "failed to alleviate"
  dyskinetic movements and noted a trend toward worsened schizophrenic symptoms
  (de Montigny et al., 1979 — PMID:117492). A double-blind trial at 2 g/day saw
  some reduction at the highest dose but concluded "further studies … are
  unwarranted" (George et al., 1981 — PMID:6942835). These are not cognition
  endpoints, but they are the most direct human tests of DMAE's purported
  cholinergic action and they are unsupportive.
- **Healthy-adult cognitive enhancement (the actual nootropic use case):**
  no adequate modern RCT demonstrating cognitive benefit **(unsourced)** — i.e.,
  the popular use is essentially unsupported by quality human data.
- **Onset, half-life, and human PK/PD for cognition:** poorly characterized
  **(unsourced)**.

## Safety & interactions

- **Teratogenicity / developmental-toxicity signal (animal).** DMAE perturbs
  choline metabolism and caused dose-dependent **neural tube defects in mouse
  embryos in vitro**, via reduced embryonic choline uptake and inhibited
  phosphatidylcholine synthesis (Fisher et al., 2002 — PMID:11919173). The NTP
  studied DMAE bitartrate prenatal developmental toxicity precisely because of
  this teratogen/reproductive-toxicant concern and widespread supplement use by
  women of childbearing age (NTP DART Report 04, 2020; introduction cites the
  mouse-embryo malformation data). **Avoid in pregnancy or possible pregnancy.**
- **Psychiatric:** trend toward worsening of schizophrenic symptoms in a deanol
  TD trial (PMID:117492) — caution in psychotic disorders.
- **General tolerability:** historically tolerated at trial doses; reported AEs
  include insomnia, headache, irritability, muscle tension/twitching, GI upset
  (older trial reports; specifics partly **unsourced**).
- **Interactions:** mechanistic competition with choline at the BBB
  (PMID:727735); net cholinergic interactions in humans are **unsourced**.

## Open questions

- Does DMAE have any net cholinergic effect in the human brain, or is it
  effectively inert / mildly anticholinergic given the BBB choline-transport
  competition? (Current data lean toward null.)
- Is there any modern, adequately powered RCT of DMAE for cognition in healthy
  adults? None located — the nootropic use rests on legacy marketing, not data.
- How does the in-vitro/animal teratogenicity signal translate to realistic oral
  human exposures? (NTP report results beyond the introduction not extracted here.)
- Why does the "ACh precursor" claim persist despite the 1970s mechanistic
  refutations (PMID:850128, PMID:727735) and the 2019 PK data (PMID:31100288)?
