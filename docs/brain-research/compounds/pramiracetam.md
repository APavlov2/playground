## Summary

Pramiracetam (CI-879) is a fat-soluble racetam-class nootropic originally developed
(Parke-Davis / Cambridge Neuroscience, industry era) and studied mainly in
traumatic brain injury (TBI), anoxia, and age-associated memory impairment (AAMI).
Its best-characterized preclinical action is enhancement of sodium-dependent
high-affinity choline uptake (HACU) in the hippocampus. **Human evidence is very
sparse** — limited to a few small, old, often industry-era studies in impaired
populations; **randomized controlled-trial evidence in healthy adults is
essentially absent.** It is a **research-grade / unapproved supplement** in the US
(not FDA-approved; sold as a research chemical). Overall evidence is graded **LOW
(1)** and should be read with heavy skepticism. Note: a clinical product (Pramistar)
is marketed in some countries (e.g., Latvia/Italy) for memory deficits in aging, but
this does not establish efficacy in healthy individuals.

## Mechanism

- **High-affinity choline uptake (ach):** In rats, pramiracetam (44 and 88 mg/kg
  i.p.) significantly increased the rate of sodium-dependent HACU into hippocampal
  synaptosomes, an effect localized to hippocampus (not cortex or striatum), and was
  interpreted as acceleration of septo-hippocampal cholinergic impulse flow
  (Pugsley et al. 1983, DOI: 10.1002/ddr.430030503). HACU is the rate-limiting step
  of acetylcholine synthesis. **This is a preclinical finding;** direct cholinergic
  measurements in humans are not available.
- **Glutamatergic / other (glu):** Broader racetam literature proposes downstream
  effects on hippocampal excitatory signaling, but a specific, validated
  glutamatergic mechanism for pramiracetam is not established (unsourced).
- Pramiracetam is highly lipophilic and crosses the blood-brain barrier; it is
  largely renally excreted with a variable elimination half-life (~2-8 h)
  (Auteri et al. 1992, PMID: 1473879; Chang et al. 1985, PMID: 4008675).

## Evidence

**Impaired populations (sparse, old):**
- TBI / anoxia: A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study in young males
  with memory/cognitive problems from head injury and anoxia (pramiracetam sulfate
  400 mg TID) reported clinically significant improvement in memory, especially
  delayed recall, with gains maintained through an 18-month open-label phase and a
  1-month post-discontinuation follow-up (McLean et al. 1991, PMID: 1786500). This is
  a **small, single, industry-era trial** — low generalizability and high risk of bias.
- Age-associated memory impairment (AAMI) / aging-related memory and attention
  deficits: pramiracetam (as Pramistar) is marketed for these indications in some
  countries, and small pilot studies in elderly subjects have been described, but
  primary peer-reviewed PMIDs were not confirmed in this review — **mark as
  (unsourced)** pending verification.

**Healthy adults:**
- **No randomized controlled-trial evidence of cognitive enhancement in healthy
  adults was identified (absent / unsourced).** Pharmacokinetic studies in healthy
  volunteers exist (PMID: 1473879; PMID: 4008675) but assessed drug disposition, not
  cognitive outcomes.

**Gaps:** No modern, adequately powered, independent RCTs; no dose-response data in
healthy populations; no long-term safety data; effect sizes and durability outside
the single TBI trial are unknown (unsourced).

## Safety & interactions

- **Regulatory/legal:** Research-grade, **not FDA-approved** in the US; sold as a
  research chemical with no guarantee of purity or identity. Approved as a drug
  (Pramistar) only in select countries.
- **Contraindications (precautionary, unsourced):** severe renal impairment (renal
  clearance, not formally studied); pregnancy/lactation (no data).
- **Interactions (mechanistic inference, unsourced):** additive cholinergic load with
  choline donors (CDP-choline, alpha-GPC); theoretical opposition with anticholinergics.
- **Notable risks:** anecdotal headache, insomnia, and agitation (not from controlled
  trials, unsourced); unknown long-term safety in healthy adults.

## Open questions

- Are the 1991 TBI/delayed-recall findings reproducible in modern, independent RCTs?
- Is there *any* validated benefit in healthy adults, or is all human evidence confined
  to impaired/aging populations?
- What is the correct, evidence-based dose and dosing schedule (if any) for cognition?
- Does the preclinical HACU effect translate to measurable human cholinergic changes?
- Long-term safety, tolerance, and purity/identity concerns of research-grade material.
- Confirm primary PMIDs/DOIs for the AAMI/Pramistar pilot studies (currently unsourced).
