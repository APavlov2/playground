## Summary

Oxiracetam is a pyrrolidinone ("racetam") nootropic, structurally a hydroxylated analog of piracetam, with mild stimulant-like / activating character. It was developed and studied in Europe (notably Italy) in the 1980s-1990s for dementia and vascular cognitive impairment and is approved/marketed in some Asian and European markets, but it is **not an FDA-approved drug in the US**; it circulates as a research-grade / unapproved supplement. **Flag:** legal and quality status is jurisdiction-dependent and consumer-grade product purity is not assured.

Evidence honesty: the human evidence base is **old** (predominantly 1980s-90s), concentrated in **dementia / multi-infarct (vascular) cognitive impairment**, with **mixed and modest** results and notable methodological/quality concerns (small samples, heterogeneous endpoints, older trial standards). There is **essentially no controlled evidence in healthy adults**. Overall grade is LOW (evidence_overall = 2).

## Mechanism

- **Glutamatergic (glu):** Oxiracetam behaves as a positive allosteric modulator of AMPA-type glutamate receptors and enhances excitatory/glutamatergic transmission and hippocampal field potentials in preclinical models, a mechanism shared across the racetam class. Human-confirmed receptor-level data are lacking (preclinical only).
- **Cholinergic (ach):** In vivo rat microdialysis shows increased hippocampal acetylcholine release, and oxiracetam counteracts scopolamine-induced cholinergic/behavioral deficits (Spignoli & Pepeu 1987, PMID: 3659072). The cholinergic effect is plausibly **indirect**, downstream of enhanced glutamatergic drive on cholinergic interneurons.
- **Cerebral blood flow (cbf):** In rodent chronic cerebral hypoperfusion models, (S)-oxiracetam (the active enantiomer) increases cerebral blood flow and reverses cognitive deficits (PMID: 28855592; PMID: 1772453). **No quantitative human CBF data** were identified — this channel is graded preclinical/lowest.

## Evidence

**Old dementia / vascular cognitive impairment trials (mixed, modest):**

- Maina et al. 1989 (PMID: 2693996) — double-blind, placebo-controlled, ~289 patients with primary degenerative and multi-infarct dementia. Reported oxiracetam **superior to placebo** (p < 0.01) on three main efficacy measures (IPSC-E, Blessed Dementia Scale, NMIC), well tolerated. Largest positive trial but by older standards.
- Villardita et al. 1987 (PMID: 3479527) — double-blind, placebo-controlled, 40 outpatients with mild-to-moderate dementia, 90 days, 1600 mg/day. Reported **improvements** on MMSE, Auditory Continuous Performance, Block Tapping, Word Fluency, and IADL. Very small sample.
- Green et al. 1992 (PMID: 1444879) — double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in Alzheimer's disease (n = 24). **Negative**: oxiracetam ineffective at reducing cognitive impairment; no group- or individual-level improvement on a broad neuropsychological battery.

Net read: signal is stronger (and itself weak/old) in **vascular/multi-infarct** dementia than in Alzheimer's disease, where a careful trial was frankly negative. Trials are small-to-moderate, heterogeneous in endpoints, and predate modern reporting standards.

**Healthy adults:** No adequately controlled studies of cognitive enhancement in healthy adults were identified — this use is **(unsourced)** and not supported by direct evidence.

**Gaps (unsourced):** modern RCTs; pharmacokinetic parameters in humans (exact half-life) (unsourced); healthy-adult dosing (unsourced); long-term safety beyond ~1 year in dementia populations (unsourced); human CBF quantification (unsourced); validated drug-interaction profile (unsourced).

## Safety & interactions

- **Tolerability:** Across dementia trials oxiracetam was generally reported as well tolerated with side-effect rates similar to placebo. As a mildly activating racetam, **insomnia, restlessness, or agitation** are the most relevant practical risks; **headache, nausea, and GI upset** are reported in the class.
- **Renal clearance:** Eliminated largely unchanged by the kidney; **caution / relative contraindication in significant renal impairment** due to accumulation (mechanistic caution).
- **Pregnancy/lactation:** No safety data — avoid.
- **Interactions:** No well-characterized human drug-drug interaction studies identified (unsourced); a theoretical additive cholinergic effect with acetylcholinesterase inhibitors or other procholinergic agents is plausible but unproven (unsourced).
- **Regulatory/quality flag:** Unapproved as a drug in the US; sold as a research-grade supplement of variable purity. Treat consumer-grade material accordingly.

## Open questions

- Does any benefit exist in **healthy adults**, or is the (weak) effect confined to impaired/deficient cerebrovascular populations? Currently unsupported.
- Is the vascular-dementia signal real and replicable under modern RCT standards, or an artifact of older, small, heterogeneous trials?
- Human pharmacokinetics (true half-life, optimal dosing schedule) and human CBF effects remain unquantified (unsourced).
- Enantioselectivity: preclinical data point to (S)-oxiracetam as the active form — is the racemic consumer product suboptimal? (unsourced in humans).
- Long-term safety and interaction profile in non-dementia users is essentially uncharacterized.
