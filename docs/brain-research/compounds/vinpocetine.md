## Summary

Vinpocetine is a semisynthetic derivative of vincamine (a vinca alkaloid) marketed as a prescription cerebral vasodilator (Cavinton) in parts of Europe and Asia and sold as a dietary supplement elsewhere. Its proposed nootropic actions rest on increasing cerebral blood flow and neuroprotection rather than direct neurotransmitter modulation. Despite decades of use, high-quality evidence for cognitive benefit is weak: the Cochrane review found the evidence for dementia inconclusive and insufficient to support clinical use (PMID 12535455). Cognition grade here is LOW-MODEST (evidence_overall 2), with any signal concentrated in vascular/post-stroke contexts rather than healthy enhancement.

**SAFETY FLAG (prominent):** The US FDA warned in June 2019 that vinpocetine may cause miscarriage or harm a developing fetus and advised pregnant women and women who could become pregnant not to take it. FDA also questioned whether vinpocetine is lawfully marketed as a US dietary supplement. Treat pregnancy as a contraindication.

## Mechanism

- **Cerebral blood flow (cbf, primary):** Vinpocetine inhibits phosphodiesterase type 1 (PDE1), raising intracellular cAMP/cGMP and lowering vascular smooth-muscle Ca2+, producing selective cerebral vasodilation and increased regional perfusion with improved O2/glucose utilization. It additionally blocks voltage-gated Na+ channels.
- **Inflammation / neuroprotection (inflam, possible):** Independently of PDE inhibition, vinpocetine inhibits NF-kB-dependent proinflammatory signaling through an IKK-dependent mechanism (PMID 20448200). Na+-channel block reduces neuronal Ca2+ influx and excitotoxicity. These neuroprotective/anti-inflammatory effects are largely preclinical (cell/animal/ischemia models) and not established as drivers of cognitive benefit in healthy humans.

Effects on classical neurotransmitter systems (ach, da, ser, glu, gaba, ne) and on mito/ntrophic/glymph/hpa are not well supported as primary mechanisms — (unsourced) for direct cognitive relevance and therefore omitted from channels.

## Evidence

- **Cochrane (dementia) — inconclusive:** Szatmari & Whitehouse pooled three small, short, double-blind RCTs and concluded the evidence for benefit in dementia is inconclusive and does not support clinical use; adverse effects appeared few at studied doses (PMID 12535455; DOI 10.1002/14651858.CD003119). This is the anchor for the conservative grade.
- **Alzheimer's RCT — negative:** A double-blind trial found vinpocetine ineffective at improving cognition or slowing decline in Alzheimer's disease (Thal et al., PMID 2715559; DOI 10.1111/j.1532-5415.1989.tb05682.x).
- **Vascular / post-stroke cognition:** Mechanistic rationale (perfusion, neuroprotection) and some regional clinical use exist for vascular cognitive impairment and post-stroke recovery, but pooled high-quality efficacy data remain limited; protocols/reviews have been registered to assess post-stroke cognitive dysfunction. Robust confirmatory RCT evidence in this population is (unsourced) at the level needed to raise the grade.
- **Healthy enhancement:** No adequate RCT base supports cognitive enhancement in healthy adults — (unsourced).

Net: cbf evidence graded 2 (plausible mechanism + impaired-population signal, weak trials); inflam graded 2 (mechanistically supported but preclinical). Overall cognition grade 2.

## Safety & interactions

- **Pregnancy (contraindication, flag prominently):** FDA (June 3, 2019) warned that vinpocetine may cause miscarriage or harm fetal development, based on National Toxicology Program data; pregnant women and women who could become pregnant are advised not to use it.
- **Regulatory status:** Vinpocetine is a prescription drug (Cavinton) in some countries and is NOT FDA-approved in the US. FDA has questioned whether it is lawfully marketed as a US dietary supplement, noting it was investigated as a drug. (FDA statement URL in sources.)
- **Anticoagulant/antiplatelet interaction:** Theoretical additive bleeding/antiplatelet risk; caution with warfarin, aspirin, and other antithrombotics. Avoid around surgery.
- **Hemodynamic:** Additive hypotension possible with antihypertensives or other vasodilators.
- **Common AEs:** Generally mild — headache, facial flushing, GI upset, transient hypotension, sleep disturbance.

## Open questions

- Does vinpocetine produce clinically meaningful cognitive benefit in well-defined vascular cognitive impairment or post-stroke cohorts under adequately powered modern RCTs?
- Are perfusion/anti-inflammatory mechanisms translatable to durable cognitive endpoints, or only acute hemodynamic changes?
- What is the actual human teratogenic risk magnitude and exposure threshold given the NTP findings?
- Given heterogeneous, non-standardized supplement products, what is the true exposure and quality control in the US market amid FDA's lawfulness concerns?
