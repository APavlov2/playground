## Summary

Centrophenoxine (meclofenoxate; Lucidril) is a legacy nootropic that is an
**ester of DMAE and para-chlorophenoxyacetic acid (pCPA).** It is **research-grade
in many markets and a prescription drug (e.g., Lucidril/Helfergin) in some
countries.** Its reputation rests on (1) **old (1970s–1990s) trials in elderly /
demented populations** suggesting a modest memory signal, and (2) a robust
**preclinical** story of **reducing neuronal lipofuscin ("age pigment") and
oxidative damage** in aged animals. **Human evidence is old and limited, and
healthy-adult RCT evidence is essentially absent.** It inherits DMAE's mechanistic
disputes and cautions. **Graded LOW (2).**

## Mechanism

- **ach (DMAE-derived, disputed).** Centrophenoxine hydrolyzes to release **DMAE**
  plus pCPA. The cholinergic/"choline-related" framing is inherited from DMAE's
  marketed (and disputed/unsupported) role as an acetylcholine precursor — see the
  DMAE entry for why that mechanism is contradicted by the better mechanistic
  data. Included here only to flag the historical claim; **graded 2, direction
  "modulate"** rather than a confirmed up-regulation.
- **inflam (antioxidant / anti-lipofuscin, preclinical).** The better-supported
  pharmacology is **antioxidant and anti-lipofuscin.** In aged rodents and neuronal
  cultures, centrophenoxine **reduces lipofuscin accumulation and lipid
  peroxidation** in cortex and hippocampus and is proposed to improve membrane
  fluidity / intracellular hydration via free-radical scavenging. This is **a
  preclinical pathway** — no human cognitive endpoint has been tied to it. Graded
  **2** (strong preclinical only), direction "down" (it lowers lipofuscin/oxidative
  load).

## Evidence

Channel grades (1–4 honest scale; this compound is deliberately low):

| Channel | Mechanism | Grade | Population | Direction |
|--------|-----------|-------|------------|-----------|
| ach | DMAE-derived cholinergic (disputed) | 2 | impaired/elderly (modest signal); mechanism disputed | modulate (claimed↑, not confirmed) |
| inflam | antioxidant / lipofuscin reduction | 2 | preclinical (aged rodents, neuronal culture) | down |

Human trials — **all old, all in elderly/impaired populations, mixed quality:**

- **Healthy independent elderly — modest, selective memory signal.** A double-blind
  study of meclofenoxate in elderly subjects found the drug **"appears to increase
  the consolidation of new information into long-term memory, but does not affect
  other aspects of remembering,"** with more drug-group subjects reporting increased
  mental alertness (Marcer & Hopkins, 1977 — PMID:329662). A single, small, dated
  trial: a narrow benefit (delayed/consolidation) against many null measures.
- **Demented elderly — biochemical/body-composition endpoints, weak cognitive
  read.** A double-blind trial in demented elderly (centrophenoxine ~2 g/day)
  reported a **significant rise in average intracellular water content** and some
  psychometric improvement, attributed to free-radical-scavenger action (Fülöp et
  al., 1990 — PMID:15374499). The primary reported endpoints are biochemical/body
  composition, not a robust cognitive outcome.
- **Healthy-adult cognitive enhancement (the actual nootropic use case):** no
  adequate modern RCT demonstrating cognitive benefit in healthy adults
  **(unsourced)** — the popular optimization use is essentially unsupported by
  quality human data.
- **Onset, half-life, and human PK/PD for cognition:** poorly characterized
  **(unsourced)**.

Preclinical (lipofuscin / oxidative aging — the strongest mechanistic leg):

- **Aged mammalian brain — learning + lipofuscin.** In aged mice, centrophenoxine
  improved learning (**fewer trials to criterion**) and **reduced neuronal
  lipofuscin** in cortex and hippocampus (Nandy, 1978 — PMID:342588).
- **Neuronal culture — direct lipofuscin reduction.** In primary rat cerebral-
  hemisphere neuronal culture, centrophenoxine (and chlorpromazine) **significantly
  reduced lipofuscin accumulation**, an effect specific to neuronal cells (Ohtani &
  Kawashima, 1983 — PMID:6411484).
- **Aged rat hippocampus — electrophysiology + oxidative markers.** Centrophenoxine
  **increased multiple-unit action-potential activity in the CA3 region while
  decreasing lipofuscin and lipid peroxidation** in old rats, correlating restored
  neural activity with reduced oxidative load (Sharma et al., 1993 — PMID:8367013).
  Note: some other animal studies failed to reverse pigment accumulation, so the
  preclinical record is positive but **not uniform**.

**Why graded 2 (not higher):** the only human data are sparse, old, and confined to
elderly/impaired subjects with a narrow or biochemical endpoint; the compelling
mechanistic (lipofuscin/antioxidant) story is **preclinical**; and there is **no
healthy-adult RCT**. Per rubric, strong-preclinical-plus-limited-human caps at 2.

## Safety & interactions (research metadata)

- **Cholinergic over-stimulation.** As a DMAE-releasing, cholinergically-framed
  agent, excess effect can present as **agitation, restlessness, insomnia,
  irritability, muscle tension/tremor**, especially at higher doses (older trial
  reports; human incidence partly **unsourced**).
- **Seizure / agitation risk.** Cholinergic agents can lower seizure threshold;
  caution / avoidance is advised in **epilepsy or seizure-prone individuals**
  (human convulsion incidence **unsourced**).
- **DMAE-related teratogenicity.** The DMAE moiety perturbs choline metabolism and
  caused **dose-dependent neural tube defects in mouse embryos in vitro** (Fisher
  et al., 2002 — PMID:11919173). **Avoid in pregnancy or possible pregnancy**, and
  during lactation by extension.
- **Psychiatric caution.** Excess cholinergic tone is a theoretical concern in
  **psychotic, bipolar, and major depressive disorders, and in Parkinson's
  disease** (mechanistic caution; human data **unsourced**).
- **Interactions.** Additive risk with other **cholinergic agents** (cholinesterase
  inhibitors, choline donors) — net effect **unsourced** for human DDIs; theoretical
  interaction with **anticonvulsants** given the convulsion-threshold concern
  (**unsourced**).
- **Research-grade status.** Sold as a research chemical/supplement in some markets
  and as a **prescription drug (Lucidril, Helfergin) in others**; purity, dose, and
  formulation are not standardized. This is research metadata, not medical advice.

## Open questions

- Is there any modern, adequately powered RCT of centrophenoxine for cognition in
  **healthy adults**? None located — the optimization use rests on legacy
  elderly/impaired data and preclinical aging models.
- Does the **anti-lipofuscin / antioxidant** action (clear in aged rodents and
  neuronal culture) translate to any measurable human cognitive endpoint, or is it
  purely a histological/biochemical effect?
- How much of any human effect is the **DMAE moiety** versus the intact ester or
  pCPA, and does the disputed DMAE cholinergic mechanism apply at all in vivo?
- Why is the preclinical lipofuscin record **not uniform** (some studies fail to
  reverse pigment accumulation)? Dose, age-of-onset, and tissue dependence are
  unresolved.
- Human PK/PD (onset, half-life, brain exposure after ester hydrolysis) are poorly
  characterized **(unsourced)**.
