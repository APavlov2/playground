## Summary

Aniracetam is a fat-soluble racetam-class nootropic that acts as a positive allosteric modulator (PAM) of AMPA-type glutamate receptors. It was studied and, in several European/Japanese markets, marketed as a prescription cognition agent for dementia; it is **not an approved drug in most jurisdictions today and is sold as a research-grade / unapproved supplement** (flag: legal status varies, purity unregulated).

Honest assessment: the mechanistic (AMPA PAM) evidence is solid and reproducible in vitro, but **human cognition data are limited, old, and confined to dementia/cognitive-impairment populations**, several with probable industry origin and little independent replication. **There is essentially no healthy-adult RCT evidence** demonstrating cognitive enhancement. Overall evidence is graded **LOW (2)** — driven by preclinical mechanism plus a small, dated, mixed-result clinical literature.

## Mechanism

- **Primary (glu):** Aniracetam binds the AMPA-receptor dimer interface and slows receptor **desensitization and deactivation**, prolonging the decay and increasing the peak amplitude of fast (AMPA-mediated) excitatory postsynaptic currents. Demonstrated in Xenopus oocytes and rat hippocampal slices (PMID 1975272) and in patch-clamp/outside-out patch studies (PMID 1660156). Effect is selective for the AMPA/quisqualate response (kainate, NMDA, GABA responses unaffected). Note in-vitro potency is modest (effects above ~0.1 mM).
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Rapidly and near-completely absorbed but subject to extensive hepatic **first-pass metabolism**, giving a very short parent half-life (~0.5 h) and low systemic bioavailability. Principal metabolite **N-anisoyl-GABA** is the likely mediator of in-vivo CNS effects.
- **Secondary / downstream (preclinical only):** In rats, aniracetam (via N-anisoyl-GABA) produces **site-specific increases in cortical/limbic dopamine and serotonin** (prefrontal cortex, basolateral amygdala, dorsal hippocampus, but not striatum or nucleus accumbens shell), an effect blocked by nicotinic ACh antagonism — implicating a **cholinergic (ach) → monoaminergic (da/ser)** cascade (PMID 11282361). These are animal microdialysis findings; human translation is (unsourced).

## Evidence

**Mechanistic / preclinical (moderate, grade 3 for glu):**
- AMPA PAM action well established in vitro (PMID 1975272; PMID 1660156).
- Cholinergic and monoaminergic downstream effects are animal-only (PMID 11282361) → graded 2, population preclinical.

**Human clinical — dementia / cognitive impairment (LIMITED, OLD, MIXED):**
- **PMID 1822317** (Senin et al., 1991): randomized double-blind placebo-controlled multicentre trial, n=109 probable Alzheimer dementia, 6 months. Aniracetam group significantly better than placebo on psychobehavioural measures; placebo deteriorated. Old, single positive trial; possible industry origin; not independently replicated.
- **PMID 22070796** (Koliaki et al., 2012): prospective **open-label** (not blinded/randomized) comparative study, n≈276, aniracetam mono vs cholinesterase inhibitor vs combination over 12 months. Suggested preservation of neuropsychological parameters and emotional benefit; open design markedly weakens inference.
- Older mixed/negative reports exist (e.g., a 3-month double-blind SDAT trial finding no aniracetam-vs-placebo difference because placebo also improved; cited via PMID 3103163 — not independently re-verified here, treat as supporting "mixed").

**Healthy adults:** No identified placebo-controlled RCT demonstrating cognitive enhancement in healthy adults — **healthy-adult efficacy is (unsourced)/absent.** Subjective benefit, onset, and duration claims in the supplement community are anecdotal **(unsourced)**.

**Gaps:** modern, independent, blinded RCTs; healthy-adult data; standardized dosing/PK-PD in humans; long-term safety — all (unsourced)/missing.

## Safety & interactions

- Human safety is characterized only by old dementia trials (generally reported as well tolerated, e.g., PMID 1822317) — **not adequate to establish healthy-adult or long-term safety.**
- **Hepatic metabolism / first-pass:** caution or avoid in significant hepatic impairment ((unsourced) for formal data).
- **Interactions:** co-use with cholinesterase inhibitors was studied (PMID 22070796) but is exploratory; CYP/hepatic interaction profile in humans is not well characterized **(unsourced)**.
- **Notable risks:** unregulated supply (research-grade), unknown long-term safety, anecdotal headache/anxiety/GI/insomnia. Avoid in pregnancy/breastfeeding.

## Open questions

1. Does aniracetam improve cognition in **healthy adults** under blinded, placebo-controlled conditions? (No evidence — likely the single biggest gap.)
2. Do the in-vivo monoaminergic/cholinergic effects seen in rats translate to humans at tolerated doses?
3. Given ~0.5 h parent half-life and ~0.2% bioavailability, is **N-anisoyl-GABA** the true active species, and what is its human PK/PD?
4. Would the 1991 positive dementia signal (PMID 1822317) survive a modern, independent, adequately powered RCT?
5. Human interaction and long-term safety profile — currently (unsourced).
