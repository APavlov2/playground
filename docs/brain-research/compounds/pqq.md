## Summary

Pyrroloquinoline quinone (PQQ) is a redox-active o-quinone marketed as a mitochondrial/antioxidant nootropic supplement. Its headline mechanism — stimulation of mitochondrial biogenesis through PGC-1alpha — is well demonstrated **in cell and animal models but is largely preclinical**. Human data exist but are **sparse, small, short, and frequently industry-linked**: a handful of RCTs (typically n < 50, 8-12 weeks, 10-20 mg/day) report modest improvements in attention, working memory, processing speed, sleep, and subjective fatigue, with supporting cerebral-blood-flow (NIRS) signals. Effect sizes are small, replication is thin, and several trials involve the BioPQQ ingredient (Mitsubishi Gas Chemical) or related commercial products. **Overall evidence grade: 2 (low).** This is a plausible mechanism with preliminary, low-quality human support — not an established cognitive enhancer.

## Mechanism

- **Mitochondrial biogenesis (mito):** In mouse hepatocytes, PQQ phosphorylates CREB at Ser133, activates the PGC-1alpha promoter, and raises PGC-1alpha mRNA/protein; mtDNA content, citrate synthase and cytochrome-c oxidase activity, and oxygen respiration increase. Knockdown of CREB or PGC-1alpha abolishes the effect, establishing the pathway (Chowanadisai et al., 2010 — PMID:19861415). Additional preclinical work attributes biogenesis to SIRT1/PGC-1alpha and AMPK signaling. **All of this is cell/animal data.**
- **Antioxidant / anti-inflammatory (inflam):** PQQ is redox-cycling and scavenges reactive oxygen species; a small human crossover study found reduced plasma CRP and IL-6 and altered oxidative-stress metabolites (Harris et al., 2013 — PMID:24231099).
- **Cerebral blood flow / metabolism (cbf):** NIRS-based studies report increased prefrontal cortex blood flow and oxygen metabolism with PQQ, consistent with (but not proof of) improved cerebral energy metabolism (Itoh et al., 2016 — PMID:26782228; Baltic et al., 2024 — PMID:38908296).
- **Neurotrophic (ntrophic):** A single MCI RCT reported raised serum BDNF, but the intervention also delivered molecular hydrogen, confounding attribution to PQQ (Baltic et al., 2024 — PMID:38908296).

## Evidence

**Healthy adults:**
- Itoh et al., 2016 (PMID:26782228): double-blind RCT, **n=41 elderly healthy**, 20 mg/day BioPQQ vs placebo for 12 weeks. Smaller change in Stroop interference (selective attention/working memory) in the PQQ group; NIRS suggested increased prefrontal blood flow. **Small sample; BioPQQ (commercial) ingredient.**
- Tamakoshi et al., 2023 (PMID:36807425, DOI:10.1039/d2fo01515c): double-blind, placebo-controlled, adults aged 20-65, 20 mg/day PQQ disodium salt for 12 weeks. Younger adults (20-40) showed improved cognitive flexibility/processing/execution speed by 8 weeks; older adults (41-65) improved complex and verbal memory by 12 weeks. Published in a journal with a commercial ingredient context.
- Nakano et al., 2012 (Functional Foods in Health and Disease, **no PMID — non-PubMed-indexed journal**): **open-label, n=17**, 20 mg/day for 8 weeks. Improvements across POMS subscales (vigor, fatigue, tension-anxiety, depression), sleep inventories, and correlation with cortisol awakening response. **Uncontrolled and low quality — hypothesis-generating only.**

**Impaired (MCI):**
- Baltic et al., 2024 (PMID:38908296, DOI:10.1016/j.jnha.2024.100287): double-blind RCT, **n=34 elderly with mild cognitive impairment**, dihydrogen-PQQ mixture twice daily for 6 weeks. Significant rise in serum BDNF (P=0.01), improved ADAS-Cog orientation domain (P=0.03), increased cerebral oxygenation (P=0.005). **Confounded by co-administered molecular hydrogen — cannot isolate PQQ; small n.**

**Preclinical biogenesis:** Chowanadisai et al., 2010 (PMID:19861415) is the foundational mechanism paper (in vitro/rodent). Numerous rodent neuroprotection / Parkinson's-model studies support the mitochondrial story but do not translate to demonstrated human cognitive benefit.

**Industry-funding caveat:** Much of the human cognition literature centers on BioPQQ (Mitsubishi Gas Chemical) or related commercial products and is conducted by or with manufacturer involvement. Independent, adequately powered, long-duration human RCTs are essentially absent. Treat all human cognitive claims as preliminary.

## Safety & interactions

- **Tolerability:** Short human trials at 10-20 mg/day are generally well tolerated. Safety/toxicology assessments tested up to 60 mg/day (and up to 100 mg/day for up to 24 weeks) without serious adverse effects.
- **Adverse effects:** Mild and inconsistent — headache, sore throat, fatigue, diarrhea, heartburn, drowsiness. At least one trial reported slightly more adverse events on PQQ than placebo.
- **Regulatory:** PQQ disodium salt holds an FDA GRAS notice (GRN 709) and an EFSA novel-food safety opinion (2017); BioPQQ 90-day rat NOAEL of 100 mg/kg bw/day.
- **Interactions:** No well-characterized human drug interactions documented (unsourced). Pregnancy/lactation safety and long-term/high-dose chronic use are not characterized (unsourced).

## Open questions

- Does PQQ improve cognition in **healthy** adults beyond small, short, often industry-linked trials? Independent replication is lacking.
- Are the cerebral-blood-flow (NIRS) and BDNF signals real, dose-dependent, and PQQ-specific (vs confounders like co-administered hydrogen)?
- Does the robust preclinical PGC-1alpha mitochondrial-biogenesis mechanism actually occur in **human brain** at supplement doses, or is human benefit (if any) driven by something else?
- Human pharmacokinetics (half-life, CNS penetration, effective brain exposure) are poorly characterized.
- Long-term safety and the relevance of PQQ's redox-cycling (pro- vs antioxidant) behavior at chronic dosing remain unresolved.
