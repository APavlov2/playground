## Summary

Schisandra chinensis is a dibenzocyclooctadiene-lignan-bearing botanical (active constituents: schisandrins / schisandrin B / schizandrin A, collectively "schisandrins") traditionally used as an adaptogen. For brain optimization, the neuro-relevant signals are concentrated in three channels — HPA/stress (adaptogen framing), neuroinflammation/oxidative stress (inflam), and cholinergic/AChE modulation (ach) — but the strength of evidence is **LOW (overall grade 2)**.

Honest appraisal: the great majority of neuro evidence is **preclinical** (rodent and cell-culture models showing antioxidant, anti-neuroinflammatory, neuroprotective, and AChE-modulating effects). Human cognition data are **few and small**, and the most-cited positive human trial used a **fixed combination** (ADAPT-232: Schisandra + Rhodiola rosea + Eleutherococcus), so any benefit cannot be attributed to Schisandra alone (combination-confound). There is no robust monotherapy human cognitive RCT. Practical concerns: CYP3A modulation (a documented, clinically significant drug-interaction risk) and hepatic effects.

## Mechanism

- **Antioxidant / anti-neuroinflammatory (inflam):** Schisandra lignans show broad antioxidant activity. Schizandrin A down-regulates LPS-induced NO, TNF-alpha and IL-6 in microglia and inhibits the TRAF6-NF-kB and Jak2-Stat3 signaling pathways, reducing microglia-mediated neuroinflammatory injury (PMID 26919063). Mechanism characterized in vitro/in vivo only.
- **Cholinergic / AChE (ach):** In scopolamine-challenged mice, schisandrin B suppressed the scopolamine-induced increase in acetylcholinesterase activity and preserved acetylcholine levels, alongside reduced oxidative stress, with improved passive-avoidance and Morris water maze performance (PMID 21615274). Preclinical.
- **Adaptogen / HPA (hpa):** Schisandra is conventionally grouped with adaptogens proposed to modulate stress responses and mental fatigue; the human signal derives from combination formulas, not isolated mechanistic human data (PMID 20374974). Precise human HPA-axis mechanism for Schisandra alone is (unsourced).
- A review collates the neuroprotective and cognitive-enhancement properties of Schisandra lignans across models (PMID 29136774, review).

## Evidence

**Preclinical neuro (the bulk of the evidence):**
- Schisandrin B prevented scopolamine-induced memory deficits in mice, suppressing AChE elevation and oxidative stress (PMID 21615274). Rodent model.
- Schizandrin A inhibited microglia-mediated neuroinflammation via TRAF6-NF-kB and Jak2-Stat3 (PMID 26919063). Cell/in vivo model.
- Broader neuroprotective/cognitive-enhancement lignan effects summarized in a review (PMID 29136774).
- Reported cognition improvement and reduced AChE/MDA with increased SOD in ovariectomized rats (mechanistic rodent finding; specific PMID not independently verified here — (unsourced) as cited primary).

**Human (few / small / combination-confounded):**
- ADAPT-232 (Schisandra + Rhodiola rosea + Eleutherococcus), single 270 mg dose, double-blind placebo-controlled in 40 healthy women: improved attention/accuracy on the d2 Test of Attention 2 h post-dose (PMID 20374974). **Schisandra is only one of three actives — benefit cannot be isolated to Schisandra (combination-confound).** Single-dose, small, single-sex.
- A standalone, adequately powered human RCT of Schisandra monotherapy on cognition: **(unsourced)** — not located. This is the central evidence gap driving the LOW grade.

**Grade rationale:** Preclinical-dominant with only small, combination-confounded human data → capped at **2**. No channel is supported by clean human monotherapy cognitive data.

## Safety & interactions

- **CYP3A modulation (key interaction risk):** In healthy volunteers, Schisandra (S. sphenanthera) extract markedly raised tacrolimus exposure (AUC ~+164%, Cmax ~+227%) — net CYP3A4/P-gp inhibition with acute/short-course co-administration (PMID 17506780). Conversely, repeated dosing can **induce** CYP3A1/2 (mRNA, protein, activity) in rats (PMID 25091466). The interaction is **bidirectional and dose/duration-dependent**, so co-use with any narrow-therapeutic-index CYP3A4 substrate (tacrolimus, many others) is clinically hazardous and requires therapeutic drug monitoring.
- **Hepatic effects:** Activity centers on hepatic CYP enzymes; use caution in hepatic impairment. Specific hepatotoxicity signal in humans at adaptogen doses is (unsourced); the concern is mechanistic (CYP/P-gp modulation) plus first-pass effects.
- **Pregnancy / uterine:** Traditional sources describe uterine-stimulant activity; precautionary avoidance in pregnancy. Strength of this specific claim is (unsourced) beyond traditional use.

## Open questions

- Is there any standalone, placebo-controlled human RCT of Schisandra **monotherapy** on cognition or stress endpoints? (Currently unsourced.)
- Can the ADAPT-232 cognitive signal be dissected to a Schisandra-specific contribution? (Not currently possible — combination-confound.)
- Human onset and half-life / pharmacokinetics of schisandrins relevant to cognitive dosing (currently unsourced).
- Net direction of clinical CYP3A effect in real-world chronic adaptogen use (inhibition vs induction) and its safety margin.
- Magnitude and clinical relevance of hepatic effects at typical supplement doses.
- Whether preclinical AChE/anti-neuroinflammatory effects translate to any measurable human cognitive endpoint.
