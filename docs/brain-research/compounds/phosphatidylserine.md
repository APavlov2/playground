## Summary
Phosphatidylserine (PS) is an endogenous anionic membrane phospholipid enriched in neuronal membranes. Its best human cognitive evidence comes from **older adults with age-related memory complaints or mild cognitive impairment**, not healthy young people. Critically, the **strongest positive data are old and used bovine-cortex-derived PS (BC-PS)** (Crook 1991, PMID:2027477; Cenacchi 1993, PMID:8323999), which is **no longer the commercial form**; modern **soy-derived PS (SB-PS)** data are weaker and mixed, including a clearly **negative** well-designed RCT in age-associated memory impairment (Jorissen 2001, PMID:11842880) alongside positive but small/industry-linked trials (Kato-Kataoka 2010, PMID:21103034; Duan 2025, PMID:39317299). The FDA granted a **qualified** health claim for reducing cognitive dysfunction/dementia in the elderly while explicitly stating the **evidence is very limited and inconsistent**. A separate small literature shows PS **blunts the cortisol/ACTH response to exercise stress** (PMID:1325348, PMID:18662395). Because the best modern (SB-PS) cognitive data are mixed and the strong data are confounded by the discontinued BC-PS form, honest **evidence_overall = 2**.

## Mechanism
- **ntrophic (membrane phospholipid):** PS is a structural component of the inner membrane leaflet and a docking site for signalling proteins (PKC, Akt/PI3K, Raf-1). The rationale for supplementation is support of membrane composition/fluidity and membrane-associated signalling in aging neurons. This is a mechanistic/structural argument, not a measured neurotrophic outcome in humans — graded 2.
- **ach (cholinergic):** Preclinical studies (predominantly BC-PS) report reversal of age-related deficits in acetylcholine release and cholinergic neurotransmission, the proposed basis for memory benefit. Not demonstrated as a measured cholinergic mechanism in human cognition RCTs, and largely tied to the BC-PS form — graded 2, impaired population.
- **hpa (cortisol/HPA axis):** In small studies, chronic PS dosing blunts the exercise-induced rise in ACTH and cortisol (PMID:1325348 used brain-cortex PS, 800 mg/day, n=9; PMID:18662395 used soy PS, 600 mg/day, n=10). The direction is a *reduction* in stress-axis reactivity. Small samples, healthy male athletes/men only — graded 2.

## Evidence
**Bovine-cortex PS (BC-PS) — historically strongest, but discontinued form:**
- Crook et al. (Neurology, 1991, PMID:2027477): double-blind RCT, n=149 with age-associated memory impairment, 100 mg BC-PS three times daily (300 mg/day) for 12 weeks. Treated group improved vs placebo on learning/memory tasks of daily life (name-face, paragraph recall, misplaced-object recall); **largest effect in the subgroup with lowest baseline performance**. A foundational study for the FDA qualified claim.
- Cenacchi et al. (Aging Milano, 1993, PMID:8323999): double-blind multicenter RCT, n=494 elderly (65-93 y) with cognitive decline, 300 mg/day BC-PS for 6 months; statistically significant improvements vs placebo on behavioural and cognitive scales (Plutchik Geriatric Rating Scale, Buschke Selective Reminding), well tolerated. Again BC-PS.

**Soy-derived PS (SB-PS) — modern form, weaker/mixed:**
- Jorissen et al. (Nutr Neurosci, 2001, PMID:11842880): **negative** double-blind RCT, n=120 elderly (>57 y) with AAMI, 300 or 600 mg/day SB-PS for 12 weeks; **no effect** on memory or other cognitive functions vs placebo. This is the key well-controlled SB-PS trial and it failed to replicate the BC-PS results — direct evidence that source matters.
- Kato-Kataoka et al. (J Clin Biochem Nutr, 2010, PMID:21103034): double-blind RCT, n=78 elderly Japanese (50-69 y) with memory complaints, SB-PS 100 or 300 mg/day for 6 months; memory improved vs baseline **only in the low-baseline subgroup**, mainly delayed verbal recall. Small, subgroup-driven, industry-linked.
- Duan et al. (J Affect Disord, 2025, PMID:39317299): double-blind RCT, n=190 Chinese older adults with MCI, ~63 mg PS/day in a food supplement for 12 months; improvements vs placebo on arithmetic, similarities, and short-term memory (partly mediated by serum alpha-linolenic acid — i.e., the supplement was not PS-only). Lower PS dose, multi-ingredient context.
- Kang & Cui et al. meta-analysis (Korean J Food Sci Technol, 2022, DOI:10.9721/KJFST.2022.54.1.52): 9 studies (5 RCTs + 4 pre-post), 961 participants, 100-300 mg/day, 6 wk-6 mo; concluded PS benefits memory in older adults with cognitive decline. **Note:** mixes BC-PS and SB-PS and includes uncontrolled pre-post studies; this journal is **not PubMed-indexed** (DOI only), so it is cited but weighted lightly.

**HPA / cortisol-blunting (separate claim, not cognition):**
- Monteleone et al. (Eur J Clin Pharmacol, 1992, PMID:1325348): brain-cortex PS 800 mg/day for 10 days, n=9 healthy men; significantly blunted ACTH (P=0.003) and cortisol (P=0.03) responses to exercise.
- Starks et al. (J Int Soc Sports Nutr, 2008, PMID:18662395): SB-PS 600 mg/day for 10 days, n=10 healthy men; lower peak cortisol and cortisol AUC vs placebo and a higher testosterone:cortisol ratio. **Very small (n≈9-10), healthy young/active men, surrogate endocrine endpoints — not cognitive outcomes.** Grade carefully (2).

**Net:** The robust cognitive benefit is largely a BC-PS phenomenon in impaired/aging cohorts; the modern soy form has at least one solid **negative** RCT and otherwise small, subgroup-driven, or multi-ingredient positive trials. Healthy young cognitive enhancement is unsupported. Honest **evidence_overall = 2** (limited/mixed for the relevant modern form).

## Safety & interactions (research metadata)
PS is well tolerated in trials; reported adverse events (mild GI upset, occasional insomnia) are generally not above placebo (PMID:21103034; safety analysis Vakhapova et al., PMID:21711517). The historical **theoretical prion/BSE risk of bovine-cortex-derived PS** is why commercial supplements switched to **soy- or sunflower-derived** PS. Theoretical additive effects with cholinergic drugs (via proposed ACh mechanism) and with other HPA-axis modulators are plausible but undocumented (unsourced). Bleeding/anticoagulant caution is theoretical and often arises from co-formulation with omega-3, not PS itself (unsourced). Pregnancy/lactation data limited (unsourced).

## Open questions
- Do the strong **BC-PS** cognitive results transfer to the **soy/sunflower PS** actually sold today, given the negative Jorissen RCT (PMID:11842880)? Source equivalence is unresolved.
- Is benefit confined to the **low-baseline / impaired** subgroup (a recurring pattern in PMID:2027477 and PMID:21103034)?
- Any reliable cognitive effect in **healthy young** adults? Currently unsupported.
- Does exercise cortisol-blunting (PMID:1325348, PMID:18662395) translate into any meaningful performance, recovery, or chronic-stress/cognitive outcome, or is it an isolated surrogate finding in small male samples?
- Optimal modern dose (commercial products often 100 mg/day vs the 300 mg/day used in positive trials) and whether co-ingredients (omega-3, phosphatidic acid) drive reported effects.
