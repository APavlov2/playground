## Summary
Sage (*Salvia officinalis* and the near-thujone-free *Salvia lavandulaefolia* / Spanish sage) is a culinary/medicinal herb whose extracts contain **monoterpenoids that inhibit acetylcholinesterase (AChE)**, giving it a plausible cholinergic, memory-relevant mechanism. The human cognitive evidence is **promising but small**: a handful of **acute, single-dose, placebo-controlled crossover RCTs in healthy young and older adults** (Tildesley 2003 PMID:12895685; Tildesley/Kennedy 2005 PMID:15639154; Scholey 2008 PMID:18350281; Kennedy 2011 PMID:20937617) show **modest improvements in memory, attention and mood**, plus **one small mild-to-moderate Alzheimer's-disease RCT** showing benefit on ADAS-cog (Akhondzadeh 2003 PMID:12605619). All are small (n approx 20-36), mostly acute, and from overlapping research groups, so the signal is real but under-replicated. The main hard safety limit is **thujone** in *S. officinalis* essential oil (proconvulsant GABA-A antagonist) — use **standardized, low-thujone extracts**, not crude oil. Honest overall grade: **2** (a defensible 2-3; capped at 2 by small samples, acute-only designs, and limited replication).

## Mechanism
- **ach (primary) — AChE inhibition:** Both species, and isolated monoterpenoid fractions, inhibit acetylcholinesterase (and butyrylcholinesterase) in vitro, increasing synaptic acetylcholine. Kennedy 2011 (PMID:20937617) confirmed the *S. lavandulaefolia* monoterpenoid extract is a **potent inhibitor of human AChE**, composed almost entirely of monoterpenoids. This is the proposed basis for the acute memory/attention effects in healthy adults and for the rationale in Alzheimer's disease (where a cholinergic deficit exists). Graded **3, population both, direction up** — mechanistically well supported and backed by several small acute RCTs, though direct demonstration of *central* in-vivo AChE inhibition in humans is indirect.
- **inflam (secondary, preclinical) — antioxidant / anti-inflammatory:** Sage is rich in rosmarinic acid, carnosic acid and related phenolics/terpenoids with antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activity in vitro and in animals, proposed as a neuroprotective contributor. No human cognitive RCT isolates this pathway. **Preclinical only, graded 1, direction modulate.**

## Evidence
**Acute, healthy adults (primary evidence base — all small placebo-controlled double-blind crossover):**
- **Tildesley et al., 2003 (PMID:12895685):** Two trials in healthy **young** volunteers (Trial 1 n=20; Trial 2 n=24) using standardized *S. lavandulaefolia* essential oil (25-150 microlitres). The **50 microlitre dose significantly improved immediate word recall** in both trials; benefits on delayed recall at 1 h and 2.5 h. First systematic evidence of acute cognitive modulation by sage.
- **Tildesley/Kennedy et al., 2005 (PMID:15639154):** Healthy young adults (n=24), *S. lavandulaefolia* essential oil 25/50 microlitres vs placebo. Both doses improved **"Speed of Memory"**; 50 microlitre also improved **"Secondary Memory"** and enhanced mood (alertness, calmness, contentedness). Confirms acute mood + cognition modulation.
- **Scholey et al., 2008 (PMID:18350281):** Healthy **older** adults (n=20, mean age ~73, all >65), dried-leaf *S. officinalis* extract. The **333 mg dose significantly enhanced secondary memory at all post-dose times** and improved accuracy of attention — interpreted as benefit to stimulus processing/consolidation rather than retrieval. Extends the signal to an aging population.
- **Kennedy et al., 2011 (PMID:20937617):** Healthy adults (n=36), *S. lavandulaefolia* monoterpenoid extract (50 microlitres). Improved **secondary memory and attention (peak ~1 h)** and reduced mental fatigue / increased alertness at 4 h; the extract confirmed as a **potent human-AChE inhibitor**. Directly links the AChE mechanism to behavioural benefit.

**Alzheimer's disease (impaired):**
- **Akhondzadeh et al., 2003 (PMID:12605619):** Double-blind RCT, n=36 mild-to-moderate AD, fixed *S. officinalis* alcoholic extract (60 drops/day) vs placebo for 16 weeks. At 4 months sage produced **significantly better ADAS-cog and CDR-SB** outcomes than placebo (ADAS-cog P=0.03; CDR-SB P<0.003), with no excess side effects (agitation actually more frequent on placebo). Single, small, positive AD trial — encouraging but not confirmatory.

**Net grade rationale:** Consistent direction of effect across **four small acute healthy-adult RCTs** plus **one small AD RCT**, with a coherent AChE mechanism, supports a grade above 1. But samples are small (n approx 20-36), most designs are single-dose/acute (chronic efficacy in healthy people largely untested), and the trials come from a small set of overlapping investigators with limited independent replication. Honest **evidence_overall = 2** (reasonable readers could argue 3). The inflam channel is preclinical only (graded 1). **Healthy vs AD should be kept distinct:** acute memory/attention/mood nudges in healthy people are *not* the same as disease-modifying benefit in AD.

## Safety & interactions
The dominant hard limit is **thujone**, an alpha/beta-thujone GABA-A receptor antagonist present in *S. officinalis* essential oil. At **high or prolonged doses** thujone is **proconvulsant/neurotoxic** (restlessness, tachycardia, vertigo, tremor, seizures). Mitigations:
- **Use standardized, low-thujone extracts**, not crude/undiluted essential oil; never ingest essential oils in large volume. ***S. lavandulaefolia* (Spanish sage) is naturally near-thujone-free** and is the preferred species for cognitive use.
- **Seizure/epilepsy:** caution or avoid high-dose thujone-containing oil (GABA-A antagonism lowers seizure threshold).
- **Pregnancy and lactation:** avoid medicinal/essential-oil doses — thujone is an emmenagogue/abortifacient (culinary food amounts are a separate matter).
- **AChE-inhibitor stacking:** the cholinergic mechanism is **additive** with donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine and huperzine A — do not assume it is safe to combine; theoretical additive cholinergic effects.
- **Anticholinergic drugs** (sedating antihistamines, TCAs, oxybutynin) pharmacologically oppose/blunt sage's mechanism.
- Theoretical **glucose-lowering** (additive with antidiabetics) and **blood-pressure** effects with high intake; bleeding caution with anticoagulants is **(unsourced)** for standardized extract.

## Open questions
- Do the acute healthy-adult effects **persist with chronic dosing**, and do they translate to real-world functional benefit, or are they transient single-dose nudges (PMID:12895685, PMID:15639154, PMID:18350281, PMID:20937617)?
- Will the single small **AD benefit (PMID:12605619)** replicate in larger, independent, multi-centre trials with disease-modifying endpoints?
- How much of the effect is **central AChE inhibition** versus antioxidant/anti-inflammatory or mood-mediated pathways? Direct human central-AChE evidence is lacking.
- What are the **optimal standardized formulation, dose and thujone ceiling** for chronic use, and the formal **human pharmacokinetics/half-life** (currently (unsourced))?
- Is there meaningful **independent replication** outside the originating research groups?
