## Summary

Fisetin is a dietary flavonol best known preclinically as a **senolytic** — it selectively clears senescent cells and, in mice, reduces senescence/SASP markers across tissues while extending health and lifespan (PMID: 30279143). It is also studied as an antioxidant, anti-neuroinflammatory, and pro-autophagy neuroprotectant in rodent and in-vitro models. **Human evidence for cognitive enhancement is absent.** Senolytic human trials exist but are early-stage and target non-cognitive endpoints (PK, safety, frailty, inflammation; e.g. NCT06431932). Oral bioavailability is poor. Overall evidence grade is **LOW (1)** and the story is explicitly preclinical.

## Mechanism

- **Senolysis / anti-inflammatory (inflam):** Preferentially eliminates senescent cells and lowers the senescence-associated secretory phenotype, reducing circulating/tissue pro-inflammatory signaling; acts as an antioxidant (raises glutathione, activates Nrf2) and downregulates NF-kB-driven neuroinflammation in rodent injury models (PMID: 30279143; PMID: 36299900; PMID: 35164003).
- **Neuroprotection / pro-autophagy (ntrophic):** In neurons and glia, fisetin induces autophagy via mTORC1 inhibition and TFEB/Nrf2 activation, promoting clearance of phosphorylated tau (PMID: 27112200), and supports mitochondrial function while reducing apoptosis (PMID: 36299900). These are mechanistic/animal findings, not human-validated trophic effects.

All channel directions are inferred from preclinical data; both channels are graded evidence 1 / population preclinical.

## Evidence

- **Senolytic / healthspan (mouse):** Acute or intermittent fisetin reduced senescence markers in multiple tissues and extended median and maximum lifespan in aged wild-type mice, with late-life dosing sufficient for benefit (PMID: 30279143).
- **Aging behavior/physiology (mouse):** In the rapidly aging SAMP8 model, fisetin reduced age-related deterioration in behavior and physiology (PMID: 28575152) — a model, not normal human cognition.
- **Neuroprotection / autophagy (in vitro & rodent):** Fisetin drove autophagic degradation of phosphorylated tau via TFEB/Nrf2 (PMID: 27112200); broader rodent neurodegeneration/oxidative-stress models are summarized in a 2022 review (PMID: 36299900) and a senotherapeutic-neuroprotection crosstalk review (PMID: 35164003).
- **Ongoing human trials (other endpoints):** Senolytic/aging trials such as NCT06431932 assess pharmacokinetics, safety/tolerability, and exploratory quality-of-life/cognition measures in older/multimorbid adults; results for cognition are not established. Mayo-Clinic-associated senolytic programs target frailty, skeletal, renal, and inflammatory endpoints — not cognitive enhancement.
- **Human cognitive enhancement in healthy adults:** **(unsourced)** — no published RCT demonstrates a cognitive benefit; this is a genuine evidence gap.

Note: A frequently cited fisetin/amyloid-beta cognition mouse paper (Molecular Neurobiology, 2017) has been **retracted** and is therefore deliberately not relied upon here.

## Safety & interactions

- **Bioavailability:** Fisetin has poor aqueous solubility, rapid first-pass metabolism, enzymatic degradation, and P-glycoprotein-mediated efflux; free fisetin is barely quantifiable up to ~90 min after oral dosing in animals (PMID: 35164003). Marketed unformulated doses may yield negligible free plasma exposure; novel formulations aim to improve absorption.
- **Anticoagulant/antiplatelet (theoretical):** As a flavonoid with antioxidant/antiplatelet potential, additive bleeding risk with warfarin/aspirin is plausible but **not clinically demonstrated** (unsourced).
- **Sparse human data:** Human safety rests on small and ongoing trials; long-term safety, drug-interaction, and dose-response profiles are not established.

## Open questions

- Does any clinically achievable oral dose/formulation produce meaningful CNS exposure in humans?
- Do senolytic effects translate to humans, and do they affect any cognitive domain?
- Is intermittent ("hit-and-run") dosing safe and effective long-term in humans?
- Are theoretical anticoagulant interactions real at supplement doses?
- Will ongoing trials (e.g. NCT06431932) report any cognition signal, or remain restricted to safety/PK and aging biomarkers?
