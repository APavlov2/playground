## Summary

Eleuthero (*Eleutherococcus senticosus*, "Siberian ginseng") is a classic adaptogen, botanically distinct from *Panax* ginseng. Marker compounds are the eleutherosides (notably eleutheroside B and E). Despite long traditional use for fatigue, stamina, and stress resilience, the human evidence for cognitive benefit is **weak, inconsistent, and largely confounded** by combination products. The single best-designed isolated-eleuthero stress RCT found **no benefit** over comparators. Most mechanistic support (HPA/stress-axis modulation, anti-fatigue energetics) is **preclinical**. Graded LOW overall (evidence_overall: 2).

## Mechanism

Proposed mechanisms are adaptogenic and mostly preclinical:

- **HPA / cellular stress axis (hpa):** Adaptogen reviews attribute effects to modulation of the stress response, including cortisol regulation, induction of heat-shock proteins (Hsp70), and suppression of stress-activated protein kinases (PMID 41235111). A broader review (PMID 40572479) summarizes neuroendocrine/immunomodulatory framing. These are mechanistic/preclinical signals, not human cognitive endpoints.
- **Anti-fatigue / energetics (mito):** Bioactivity-guided fractionation in mice (PMID 20920564) attributed anti-fatigue effects of eleutheroside-containing fractions to increased fat utilization, delayed blood-urea-nitrogen accumulation, and reduced lactate buildup in a weight-loaded swimming model. The mitochondrial/energetic interpretation is inferential; this is animal data only.
- **Neurotrophic (not separately graded):** The 2025 review (PMID 41235111) notes BDNF increases in some preclinical work; human neurotrophic evidence is absent (unsourced).

## Evidence

Human RCT evidence is sparse, small, and mostly tied to combination formulas.

- **No benefit in stress-related fatigue/concentration (isolated eleuthero, best design):** Schaffler, Wolf, Burkart, *Pharmacopsychiatry* 2013 (PMID 23740477; DOI 10.1055/s-0033-1347178). N=144, 120 mg/day root extract vs. stress-management training vs. combination, 8 weeks. Almost all parameters improved over time **without group differences**; eleuthero added to training was **not superior** to training alone on any parameter at week 8. This is the most direct test and is **negative** for isolated eleuthero on cognition/fatigue.
- **Combination product (cannot isolate eleuthero):** Aslanyan et al., *Phytomedicine* 2010 (PMID 20374974; DOI 10.1016/j.phymed.2010.02.005). Single-dose ADAPT-232 (a fixed combination of *Rhodiola rosea* + *Schisandra chinensis* + *E. senticosus*), N=40 healthy women, improved attention/speed/accuracy on a d2 attention test within ~2 h (p<0.05). **Eleuthero's individual contribution cannot be determined** from this design.
- **Elderly quality of life (small, transient):** Cicero et al., *Arch Gerontol Geriatr Suppl* 2004 (PMID 15207399; DOI 10.1016/j.archger.2004.04.012). N=20 elderly (hypertensive/digitalized), 300 mg/day, 8 weeks. Higher social-functioning scores at 4 weeks vs. placebo that **did not persist** to 8 weeks; no adverse events. Tiny sample, transient effect, not a cognition-specific endpoint.
- **Trial-quality caveat:** The 2025 phytochemistry/pharmacology review (PMID 41235111; DOI 10.3389/fphar.2025.1683795) concludes there is a "lack of robust clinical evidence" for asthenia, citing high heterogeneity, poor preparation standardization (43- to >200-fold eleutheroside variation), small samples, and adulteration (e.g., ~36% of UK supplements containing other *Eleutherococcus* species).

Net: one direct isolated-eleuthero cognition/fatigue RCT is **negative**; positive signals come from a multi-herb blend or a tiny transient geriatric study. Mechanism is preclinical-only. This supports a LOW grade (max 2).

## Safety & interactions

- **General tolerability:** Generally regarded as well tolerated; adverse effects are rare and poorly documented, with a lack of long-term safety data (PMID 40572479).
- **Insomnia / CNS:** Insomnia, headache, and arrhythmia have been reported infrequently, largely in older low-quality USSR-era studies (PMID 40572479). Avoid late-day dosing.
- **Blood pressure / hypertension:** WHO advises caution in hypertension; however, the hypertension "contraindication" is **disputed** and some data suggest mild antihypertensive effects (evidence inconclusive) (PMID 40572479). Treat as a precaution, not a firm contraindication.
- **Digoxin interaction:** Documented increase in serum digoxin levels with co-administration, likely via altered absorption / P-glycoprotein inhibition (PMID 40572479). Clinically relevant for narrow-therapeutic-index drugs.
- **Antidiabetic / hypoglycemia:** Hypoglycemic properties noted; possible additive effect with antidiabetic drugs and reversible drowsiness (PMID 40572479).
- **Anticoagulant / antiplatelet:** A theoretical additive bleeding risk is commonly cited but not well documented for eleuthero specifically (unsourced).

## Open questions

- Is there any cognitive benefit of **isolated, standardized eleuthero** in healthy adults? The single best RCT is negative; adequately powered, well-standardized monotherapy trials are missing.
- Do human HPA-axis or BDNF/neurotrophic effects exist, or are these preclinical-only? (currently unsourced in humans)
- Are the anti-fatigue energetic/mitochondrial mechanisms (mouse swimming models) translatable to human mental fatigue? (unsourced in humans)
- How much do product standardization/adulteration problems explain the inconsistent clinical record?
- Is the hypertension contraindication real, or should it be retired given possible antihypertensive signals?
