## Summary
Caffeine + L-theanine is a popular acute "calm focus" stack pairing caffeine (an adenosine antagonist that raises arousal/alertness) with L-theanine (a tea-derived amino acid that promotes a relaxed-but-alert state). Across several small double-blind RCTs the combination reliably improves attention-switching speed/accuracy, simple/working-memory reaction time and subjective alertness, and L-theanine appears to blunt some of caffeine's downsides (jitter, anxiety, and acute cerebral vasoconstriction). Effects are modest, acute, and the most robust component is caffeine's well-established attention benefit; importantly, much of caffeine's apparent "boost" in habitual users is reversal of overnight withdrawal rather than net cognitive enhancement.

## Mechanism

Treat this as two components with complementary actions.

- **ne (caffeine, up):** Caffeine non-selectively antagonizes adenosine A1 and A2A receptors. Adenosine accumulates with wakefulness and exerts tonic inhibition; blocking it disinhibits downstream noradrenergic, dopaminergic and cholinergic systems, increasing arousal and alertness. In habitual consumers, upregulated adenosine receptors mean a large share of the acute alertness effect is **withdrawal reversal**, not net enhancement (see Evidence). Evidence 3 (multiple RCTs / meta-analysis support arousal/attention effects).

- **da (caffeine, up):** A2A receptors form functionally antagonistic heteromers with dopamine D2 receptors in striatum; A2A blockade by caffeine effectively increases dopaminergic signalling tone, contributing to mood, motivation and caffeine's reinforcing/reward properties. Human evidence is largely indirect/mechanistic — evidence 2.

- **gaba (L-theanine, modulate):** L-theanine produces a relaxed-but-alert state indexed by increased EEG alpha-band power (Nobre 2008, PMID:18296328) and reduced subjective stress reactivity. Proposed neurochemistry includes modest increases in GABA plus modulation of glutamate. The alpha-wave/relaxation finding is reasonably supported in humans, but the *specifically GABAergic* mechanism is mechanistic/preclinical — evidence 2, direction modulate (calming without sedation).

- **glu (L-theanine, modulate):** L-theanine is a structural analogue of glutamate/glutamine and weakly modulates NMDA/AMPA receptors and glutamate transport — the putative basis of its neuromodulatory/anti-stress action. Human-level evidence is thin; mostly preclinical/mechanistic — evidence 2.

- **cbf (caffeine, down):** This direction is commonly misread, so to be precise: caffeine **acutely reduces** resting cerebral blood flow. As an adenosine antagonist it causes cerebral vasoconstriction; Dodd 2015 (PMID:25761837) measured reduced oxygenated haemoglobin and increased deoxygenated haemoglobin (NIRS), strongest in non-habitual users. L-theanine added at tea-equivalent doses attenuated/eliminated this vasoconstrictive effect. So caffeine alone = CBF down; the combination blunts that drop. Evidence 3, direction **down**.

## Evidence

Human RCTs on the **combination** (all healthy adults, acute, crossover unless noted):

- **Owen 2008 (PMID:18681988):** 27 healthy volunteers, 50 mg caffeine ± 100 mg L-theanine vs placebo. Caffeine improved subjective alertness (60 min) and attention-switching accuracy (90 min). The **combination** improved both speed and accuracy of attention-switching at 60 min and reduced susceptibility to distraction in a memory task at 60 and 90 min — i.e. the combination outperformed caffeine alone on focused attention.

- **Haskell 2008 (PMID:18006208):** Higher doses, 150 mg caffeine + 250 mg L-theanine. Caffeine alone improved digit vigilance reaction time, RVIP accuracy and reduced mental fatigue. The **combination** produced faster simple reaction time, faster working-memory reaction time, better sentence-verification accuracy, increased "alert" and reduced "tired"/"headache" ratings. L-theanine alone showed some negatives (more headache, fewer correct serial-sevens), so most benefit is driven by caffeine with theanine improving the subjective/jitter profile.

- **Giesbrecht 2010 (PMID:21040626):** 44 young adults, 97 mg L-theanine + 40 mg caffeine vs placebo. Combination improved task-switching accuracy and self-reported alertness and reduced tiredness — supports the "focused calm" claim at low, tea-realistic doses.

- **Dodd 2015 (PMID:25761837):** 12 habitual + 12 non-habitual caffeine consumers; 75 mg caffeine, 50 mg theanine, both, or placebo. Primary value here is the CBF/NIRS data (caffeine vasoconstriction, theanine attenuation). Cognitive/mood benefits at these low doses were modest, and importantly effects differed by **habituation status**, underscoring the withdrawal-reversal confound.

Supporting single-component evidence:

- **Caffeine attention meta-analysis — Kløve & Petersen 2025 (PMID:40335666):** 31 RCTs, 1,455 rested healthy adults; pure caffeine acutely improved both reaction time and accuracy on attention tasks, dose-dependently. This anchors the "ne/attention" channel at grade 3-4 for caffeine specifically.

- **L-theanine alpha waves — Nobre 2008 (PMID:18296328):** 50 mg L-theanine increased EEG alpha activity vs placebo, interpreted as relaxation without drowsiness — anchors the theanine relaxation/alertness modulation.

**Tolerance & withdrawal confound (important):** Most single-dose caffeine trials test overnight-abstained habitual users, so measured "improvement" partly reflects relief of caffeine withdrawal rather than true enhancement. Studies that pre-withdraw participants for ~a week support the withdrawal-reversal hypothesis. Tolerance develops to caffeine's arousal and CBF effects with regular use. Net: in habituated consumers, expect a smaller true cognitive gain than the raw vs-placebo numbers imply; the theanine contribution (reduced jitter/anxiety, attenuated vasoconstriction) is the more distinctive added value of the stack. Effect sizes across these RCTs are generally small-to-moderate and sample sizes are small (n≈12-44), hence **evidence_overall: 3** (multiple consistent RCTs for the combination, but small and partly confounded; not yet a combination-specific meta-analysis).

## Safety & interactions (research metadata)

- **Caffeine:** transient blood-pressure rise, tachycardia/palpitations, anxiety/jitteriness (dose-dependent), and sleep disruption if taken later in the day (long half-life). Dependence with withdrawal headache/fatigue on cessation. Caution in anxiety/panic disorders, uncontrolled hypertension/arrhythmia, and pregnancy (commonly limited to <200 mg/day). Metabolized by CYP1A2 — fluvoxamine, ciprofloxacin and oral contraceptives raise caffeine exposure; additive effects with other stimulants/methylxanthines.
- **L-theanine:** very well tolerated with a benign safety profile; no clinically significant toxicity reported at studied doses. One RCT (Haskell 2008) noted increased headache ratings with isolated high-dose theanine, but this is not a consistent finding. A weak/possible additive blood-pressure-lowering interaction with antihypertensives has been proposed but is not well established. **For practical purposes, L-theanine itself has no major documented contraindications or drug interactions** — the stack's safety profile is essentially that of its caffeine content, with theanine plausibly softening caffeine's anxiogenic and vasoconstrictive effects.

## Open questions

- How much of the combination's benefit is genuine enhancement vs withdrawal reversal? No combination RCT has cleanly pre-withdrawn habitual users and stratified by habituation across all outcomes.
- Optimal caffeine:theanine ratio and dose — studied combos vary widely (1:1 to ~1:2.5); the commonly cited ~1:2 is heuristic, not established by head-to-head dosing trials.
- Durability/tolerance of the combination's specific "calm focus" benefit with repeated daily use is essentially untested (almost all data are single-dose, acute).
- Mechanism of theanine's anti-jitter/alpha effect in humans (true GABA increase vs glutamatergic modulation vs other) remains unresolved.
- Whether theanine's attenuation of caffeine-induced CBF reduction has any meaningful cognitive or long-term cerebrovascular consequence is unknown.
- Generalizability beyond young healthy adults (older adults, clinical anxiety, ADHD) is largely unstudied for the combination.
